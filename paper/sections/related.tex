\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
%% Five clusters; each paragraph states what the line does, then the single
%% structural difference from our setting. No em-dashes; verified citations only.

\paragraph{Agent distillation.}
Recent work transfers tool-use behavior from large teachers into small
students: \citet{kang2025distilling} distill full task-solving trajectories
with retrieval and code tools, \citet{liu2025structured} apply
segment-specific losses over reasoning and action spans, and
\citet{tang2026smartad} select teacher trajectories by student likelihood to
match student capacity. All of these methods maximize aggregate benchmark
performance for a fixed dataset. None estimates the task domain from
examples, provides a boundary predictor, or evaluates whether the student's
capability exceeds the intended scope; capacity alignment in
\citet{tang2026smartad} concerns what the student \emph{can absorb}, not
what the deployment \emph{specifies}. Our setting takes the specification as
input and treats both boundary and two-sided capability match as
deliverables.

\paragraph{Gradient-based data selection and attribution.}
LESS \citep{xia2024less} selects instruction data whose sketched gradients
align with a target validation set, exploiting the observation that
zero-initialized LoRA adapters yield free random projections of full
gradients \citep{hu2022lora, johnson1984extensions}. TRAK
\citep{park2023trak} predicts per-example model behavior from projected
gradient inner products, and \citet{rosser2026gradient} discovers behavior
clusters by sparse decomposition of training gradients. These lines use
gradient geometry to diagnose or to serve a fixed target set. We repurpose
the same geometry as a \emph{task specification}: a conformally calibrated
region estimated from $k$ examples that drives selection, prediction, and
refusal jointly, with the sparse decomposition acting as the shared
representation rather than a post-hoc analysis.

\paragraph{Conformal out-of-distribution rejection.}
Split conformal prediction provides finite-sample coverage guarantees
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic}, with conditional variants sharpening
per-group validity \citep{gibbs2025conditional}. SCOPE \citep{li2026scope}
applies conformal gates to hidden activations for rejecting
out-of-distribution requests at serving time. Serving-time rejection leaves
the student's capability untouched. In our setting the same statistical
machinery calibrates a boundary that also shapes training, so the guarantee
and the student are coupled rather than layered.

\paragraph{Model reuse by specification.}
The learnware paradigm equips a pool of specialized small models with
capability specifications so that users can retrieve a model matching their
task \citep{tan2025learnware}. Retrieval presupposes that a suitable
specialist already exists. Distill-to-specification is the generative
counterpart: the specification is estimated from examples and a matching
specialist is synthesized, with the specification itself constraining
training.

\paragraph{Capability removal.}
Unlearning removes designated capabilities from trained models, recently via
sparse-autoencoder subspace projections \citep{wang2025sspu}. Removal
operates post hoc on a large model and must fight the optimizer that
installed the capability. We shape capability during distillation instead:
our mechanism analysis indicates that out-of-domain competence decays by
supply starvation when the training diet is narrowed
(Section~\ref{sec:distill}), so the shaping lever is data allocation rather
than parameter surgery. Behavioral suppression through refusal training is
complementary and we evaluate it as a separate module.
