\paragraph{Learning from black-box teachers.}
Sequence-level knowledge distillation trains a student on teacher
outputs \citep{kim2016sequence, mukherjee2023orca}, and its agentic
descendants refine what is imitated, whether with segment-specific
losses over reasoning and action spans \citep{liu2025structured},
retrospective thought prefixes \citep{kang2025distilling},
capacity-aligned trajectory selection \citep{tang2026smartad}, teacher
feedback turned into preference objectives \citep{tunstall2023zephyr,
pbsd2026}, or teacher supervision on student-visited states
\citep{ye2025bbopd}. The limits of imitation are well documented.
Small models imitating proprietary teachers acquire style rather than
capability \citep{gudibande2023false}, fine-tuning erases previously
held abilities \citep{luo2023empirical}, and agent-tuning pipelines
mix general data into training precisely to limit that erosion
\citep{zeng2023agenttuning}. White-box distillation can control what
moves by matching or reverse-matching distributions
\citep{agarwal2024gkd, gu2024minillm}, and learning-to-reweight
methods adjust example weights by a validation gradient
\citep{ren2018learning}; the former needs signals that a text-only
teacher does not expose, and the latter decides only how much each
example counts. Our transfer differs in the decision it makes. The
teacher mass of a state is fixed before sampling and is not a learned
weight; what the return decides is which of the behaviors of the
student gives way, and the component shared by those behaviors is
cancelled rather than reweighted. Self-improvement with verifiers is
the closest teacher-free line. STaR retrains on self-generated
solutions that pass a checker \citep{zelikman2022star},
rejection-sampling fine-tuning and ReST generalize this into
generate-filter-train loops \citep{yuan2023scaling, gulcehre2023rest,
singh2023beyond}, and online preference optimization shows that fresh
negatives beat frozen ones \citep{guo2024direct}. We keep fresh
samples from the student as the behaviors that can give way, and we
let the teacher supply the mass that a cold student cannot sample by
itself.

\paragraph{Valuing and selecting evidence under a budget.}
Data selection scores examples by gradient similarity to a target
\citep{xia2024less}, by influence or submodular coverage
\citep{gist2026, trim2025, icons2025}, or by quality filters
\citep{chen2024alpagasus, liu2024deita}, and active learning prices a
query by expected model change or expected error reduction
\citep{settles2009active}. All of these value an example on its own or
against a fixed target; the marginal contribution of a purchase to the
update that the already-owned evidence produces is not part of the
score. Budgeted use of paid models has been studied as cascading and
routing between cheap and expensive calls \citep{chen2023frugalgpt},
where the budget buys inference rather than supervision. Our
acquisition values a candidate by the marginal gain it adds to the
joint update of the owned evidence under the same replacement problem
that trains the student, with a hard ledger that charges failed
completions at their recorded cost and a posterior that reads only
pre-purchase information. The comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:rq3}
place this rule against the selection and generation families under
one budget cap.
