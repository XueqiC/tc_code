\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
%% Two blocks: (1) how task boundaries are determined today, (2) how LLM
%% agents are distilled today. The gap statement closes block two.

\paragraph{Determining task boundaries.}
When a served model must decide whether a request is in scope, current
practice draws the boundary in input space. Conformal methods calibrate
acceptance thresholds with finite-sample guarantees
\citep{vovk2005algorithmic, gibbs2025conditional}, and SCOPE applies such
gates to hidden activations to reject out-of-distribution requests at
serving time \citep{li2026scope}. The learnware line attaches
capability specifications to a pool of specialists so that tasks can be
routed to a matching model \citep{tan2025learnware}. A parallel line
selects instruction data by quality, diversity, or mixture weights
\citep{zhou2023lima, chen2024alpagasus, liu2024deita, xie2023doremi,
sorscher2022beyond}; these methods curate for aggregate quality and
carry no notion of a task boundary. At serving time, routers and
cascades \citep{chen2023frugalgpt, ong2024routellm} decide who answers
a request but not what a distilled student was allowed to learn.
Weight-space editing \citep{ilharco2023task, yadav2023ties} manipulates
capabilities after training without a specification to match, and
regularization against forgetting \citep{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}
protects capability rather than bounding it. All of these define the
boundary over surface representations of the query or over a
pre-existing model pool, and all of them leave the boundary decoupled
from how the served model was trained. Both choices fail structurally
in our setting. Two tasks can share identical query text while
demanding different capabilities, as Section~\ref{sec:boundary} shows, and a
serving-time gate cannot make the student's competence stop where the
boundary stops.

\paragraph{Distilling LLM agents.}
Agent distillation transfers tool-use trajectories from a large teacher
to a small student, whether with retrieval and code tools
\citep{tunstall2023zephyr, mukherjee2023orca, gu2024minillm,
agarwal2024gkd, kang2025distilling}, with segment-specific losses over
reasoning and action spans \citep{liu2025structured}, or with
trajectories selected for compatibility with student capacity
\citep{tang2026smartad}. These methods take a fixed training set as
given and maximize aggregate benchmark scores. None takes a task
specification as input, none reports what the student can or cannot do
relative to an intended domain, and capacity alignment concerns what
the student can absorb rather than what the deployment requires. The
two lines therefore never meet, since boundary methods do not shape training
and distillation methods do not know their boundary. The raw material
for a bridge exists in gradient-based analysis, where sketched
gradients select influential data \citep{koh2017understanding,
pruthi2020estimating, xia2024less, hu2022lora}, predict per-example
behavior \citep{park2023trak}, and expose behavior clusters through
sparse decomposition \citep{rosser2026gradient}. These tools have so
far been used to diagnose models or to serve fixed validation sets. We
use the same geometry as the specification itself, building a conformally
calibrated task region estimated from $k$ examples that defines the
boundary, selects the distillation data, and predicts the student's
capability, so that the guarantee and the student are trained into
agreement rather than assembled afterwards.
