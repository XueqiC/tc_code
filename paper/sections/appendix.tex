
\section{Additional methodology details}\label{app:method}

This section provides the implementation details omitted from
Section~\ref{sec:method}: the complete procedure of one decision
window, the exposure and ledger conventions, the estimator components
with the length correction, the joint solver, the acquisition labels
and predictor, and the attribution controls used to isolate the
mechanism.

\paragraph{Complete procedure.}
Training runs in budget rounds of twelve commit steps; steps 1, 4, 7,
and 10 are decision windows. A decision window executes, in this
order: (i) select and purchase up to $K$ packages with the acquisition
predictor under the remaining authorization, revealing and settling
each at its recorded cost through the ledger; (ii) fix the evidence
of the step and the exposure weights $w_i$, and draw $m=2$ fresh
source actions per unit from the frozen snapshot of the round;
(iii) score the sources and the teacher texts to obtain the hard,
soft, and teacher gradients, and assemble the teaching problem, that
is, the base update, the directions $b_{ij}$, the Gram $K$, and the
box; (iv) build the reference student $\theta^{+}(a^{\mathrm{ref}})$
in a trial from the current parameters, estimate the return gradient
$\hat h$ at it with temperature-one rollouts on the feedback fold, and
restore the parameters, optimizer state, and random state; (v) solve
the joint problem to a checked tolerance and commit
$\theta^{+}(a^{\star})$ once from the original parameters with the
frozen preconditioner. The acquisition labels and the selection share
the same problem definition; nothing is recomputed with a second
formula. Non-decision steps sample units from the accumulated pool
under the frozen rules, reuse the latest return gradient, recompute
the directions and the uncertainty terms on the new samples, and
journal the feedback age.

\paragraph{Exposure and ledger conventions.}
One exposure unit is the teacher record of one state plus two source
actions; each commit step uses $E=40$ units (eighty actions), at most
$K=20$ of them from new packages, in micro-batches of four states and
ten micro-batches per commit. Before any purchase the units are
reference-pool states without teacher evidence, for which every share
is zero. The ledger keeps reserved plus settled cost at or below the
authorization; a package is reserved at its public cap, revealed,
settled at its recorded cost, and the remainder released; the same
package is paid once; a failed completion is charged. The budget
denominator is the recorded content cost of the sealed pool, and the
authorizations at the ends of the two rounds are ten and twenty-five
percent of it. The support tasks are split into two folds that rotate
between rounds; the inner fold supplies source states and candidates,
and the feedback fold supplies returns only and never enters a
gradient, the preconditioner, or any normalization.

\paragraph{Estimator components and length correction.}
Sources are complete behaviors with their true termination token or
the true cap, never re-sampled for diversity and never filtered by
format or correctness. For a source $Y$ of realized length $L$ with
prefixes $h_t$, let $g_t(v)$ be the gradient of the negative
log-probability of token $v$ at prefix $h_t$ and
$s_t=\sum_v p_t(v\mid h_t)\,g_t(v)$ the snapshot-averaged gradient at
that prefix. Under total-token normalization the hard gradient is
$G_{\Sigma}=\sum_{t\le L}g_t(Y_t)$ and the soft retention gradient
$S_{\Sigma}=\sum_{t\le L}s_t$ has the same expectation, because the
event of reaching prefix $t$ is measurable with respect to the past;
it vanishes at $\pi_\theta=p_t$. Under per-sequence normalization the
hard gradient is $G_{\Sigma}/L$, and the factor $1/L$ depends on the
current and future tokens, so $S_{\Sigma}/L$ is biased. We use the
length-corrected estimator
$G^{\mathrm{soft}}=cS_{\Sigma}+(1/L-c)\,G_{\Sigma}$ with a constant
$c$ fixed before sampling, so that
$G^{\mathrm{soft}}-G_{\Sigma}/L=c\,(S_{\Sigma}-G_{\Sigma})$ has zero
expectation with no random multiplier; $c=1/1024$ throughout. Hard
sources and teacher texts are each normalized by their own token
count, and the teacher length includes its native turn terminator.
The identity holds for every measurable, frozen choice of the shares.
Generation-time per-token log-probabilities are checked against
teacher-forced rescoring under a fixed tolerance, so that a sample
whose recorded scores disagree with the model is rejected before it
enters any gradient.

\paragraph{Joint solver.}
With $x=a-a^{\mathrm{ref}}$ and auxiliary variables $t\ge|x|$, the
problem of Section~\ref{sec:distill} is the convex program
$\min\ \tfrac{1}{2}\lambda x^{\top}Kx-(U^{\top}\hat h)^{\top}x
+\varepsilon^{\top}t$ subject to $t\ge x$, $t\ge -x$, and the box on
$x$. $K$ is symmetrized and its negative eigenvalues within a relative
tolerance are clipped to zero, with the minimum eigenvalue, the
asymmetry residual, and the repair norm recorded; a significantly
indefinite $K$ is an error. The program is solved by a sequential
quadratic method, the non-negative multipliers are recovered, and the
solution is accepted only when primal feasibility, stationarity, and
complementary slackness hold to the configured tolerance after
division by a common positive scale of the objective. $\lambda$ is
configured in the original units of $K$; no implicit rescaling by the
mean diagonal is applied. $\varepsilon_\ell$ is the leave-one-out
standard error of $\hat h^{\top}b_\ell$ over the feedback rollouts and
is labeled an uncertainty regularizer. $a^{\mathrm{ref}}=\tfrac12$ for
the first teacher version of every source and $0$ for any later
version; the first window of each run uses the reference shares.
Journaled per step: $U^{\top}\hat h$, $\varepsilon$, the spectrum
statistics of $K$, $a^{\star}$, the active constraints, the objective
value, the parameter increment error against the affine prediction,
and the feedback age.

\paragraph{Return gradient.}
For each feedback task $t$ with independent full trajectories
$\tau_{tk}$ and fixed task weights $\omega_t$,
$\hat h=\sum_t\frac{\omega_t}{n_t}\sum_k(R_{tk}-b_{tk})\,\nabla\sum_{u}
\log\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}}(y_u\mid s_u)$, the sum over the actions
of the student in the trajectory; tool outputs and environment text
are conditioning, not scored actions. The baseline $b_{tk}$ is the
leave-one-out mean of the other trajectories of the same task, which
does not depend on the current trajectory, or a fixed zero. Rollouts
are generated at temperature one and top-$p$ one by the same backend
that scores them. Only the projections $U^{\top}\hat h$ and their
standard errors are stored.

\paragraph{Acquisition labels and predictor.}
For a purchased package the label is
$F^{\star}(\mathcal{D}\cup\{q\})-F^{\star}(\mathcal{D})$ computed at
the same reference, with the same $\hat h$, and with the old weights
unchanged, by appending the revealed directions to the same problem
and re-solving it. A source slot supporting several teacher versions
carries shares $a_{ijv}\ge0$ with $\sum_v a_{ijv}\le1$; a new version
enters with reference share zero, so setting it to zero recovers the
old problem, its optimum, and its parameter increment exactly. The
predictor is a small model over pre-purchase features only, namely the
public features of the package, free student behavior at its state,
the request cap, and a summary of the current teaching problem and of
the evidence already owned; it reads no unpurchased text, no teacher
gradient, no verification result, and no realized output length, and
it is validated on purchases grouped by parent task. The control arm
replaces it by a random draw under the same budget and cost model.
Within a window, candidates are first screened by the marginal
derivative of the current objective with respect to a new direction
and only a short list is re-solved.

\paragraph{Attribution controls.}
On one purchased pool and one recorded exposure schedule, the arms of
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} share initialization, samples, feedback
protocol, optimizer, rank, and step count and differ only in the
control: D0 trains teacher SFT plus a source-prefix KL to the snapshot
on the same rows; D1 is the earlier block procedure with a state-only
gate and a separate split solver; D2 adds the equality
$a_{i1}=a_{i2}$ to the joint problem; D3 is the full problem; the
variants drop the off-diagonal of $K$ in the solver only, replace the
control-variate estimator by the raw one, permute the source pairing
after solving, or fix the mean share per slot and free the split. The
restriction or intervention is recorded in the run manifest, and the
shuffled or diagonal solutions are never reported as optima of the
full problem.

\paragraph{Optimization and implementation constants.}
Students train LoRA adapters (rank 16, $\alpha_{\mathrm{LoRA}}=32$,
dropout 0) with AdamW at learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$; the
preconditioner $P$ used for the virtual reference and the directions
$v_i$ is the diagonal preconditioner of the optimizer accumulated on
the inner fold before any purchase. Each commit step uses $E=40$
exposure units, $K\le 20$ new packages, micro-batches of four states,
and ten micro-batches per commit; two source actions per unit at
temperature one, top-$p$ one, resampled at every step; the action cap
is 256 tokens on ALFWorld, 128 on WebShop, and the harness cap on
BFCL. Return
feedback uses the feedback fold with a fixed number of temperature-one
rollouts per task and leave-one-out standard errors; $\lambda=1$ on
the normalized interaction matrix; warm-up $d=0$ for the first window;
gate features are state-only with a scalar variant
$\alpha=\sigma(\phi_0)$; the control arm fixes $\alpha=0.5$. The
acquisition posterior is shrunk to five percent of its mean until four
windows and eight labels show positive out-of-window predictive power;
public cost caps are 2{,}048 output tokens for demonstration packages
and 512 for generator packages, certified against the sealed pool.
Generation batches at most eight prompts within a padded-token budget;
teacher-forced scoring is unbatched. Experiments run on a shared lab node with mixed NVIDIA GPUs (RTX PRO
6000 96GB, A100 80GB, RTX 6000 Ada 48GB); all arms of a comparison run
on one hardware class.

\paragraph{Budget accounting.}
$B$ is a cap on teacher output tokens per benchmark, applied
identically to every teacher-using method; the actual spend of each
method is reported next to its score. Charged items are teacher
demonstrations including discarded failures, teacher ranking outputs
(dDPO), and teacher corrections on student-visited states (on-policy
correction). Student rollouts, environment interaction, verification,
and GPU time are reported separately and are not part of $B$. Where a
method trains from the sealed demonstration pool of the acquisition
experiments, its cost is the recorded output-token cost of the pool
content it uses.

\paragraph{Baseline implementations.}\label{app:baselines}
All baselines train the same LoRA student (rank 16) on the same
support set with identical optimization constants, and differ only in
the training pool and loss. Losses are computed on assistant-generated
spans only; environment observations and prompts are masked. The
branches follow the black-box taxonomy of \citet{xu2024kdsurvey}.
\textbf{SFT} \citep{mukherjee2023orca} minimizes the
token-level NLL on verified teacher trajectories.
\textbf{SAD} (Structured Agent Distillation, \citealp{liu2025structured})
trains on the same pool with reasoning and action spans weighted
separately in the NLL; the teacher-distribution alignment of the
original method is not available from a black-box teacher and is
replaced by hard labels. \textbf{BBOPD}
(black-box on-policy distillation, adapted from \citealp{ye2025bbopd}) lets the rollouts of the student
itself fix the visited states, queries the teacher once per visited
state for the action it would take there, and trains the resulting
(state, teacher action) pairs by NLL; this keeps the on-policy state
distribution of black-box on-policy distillation but not its
discriminator or adversarial objective, and on a single-turn benchmark
the visited state is the query itself, so the pool coincides with the
demonstration pool. \textbf{dDPO} (distilled DPO,
\citealp{tunstall2023zephyr}) runs an SFT stage followed by
DPO whose preference pairs are the samples of the student ranked best
and worst by the teacher; the frozen initial student is the DPO
reference model, and the ranking outputs are charged to $B$.
\textbf{PBSD (offline pref.)} (an offline variant of
\citealp{pbsd2026}) pairs teacher positives with the failed first turn
of the student as the rejected side and trains jointly with the
positive NLL; unlike PBSD it uses a static external teacher and a
fixed negative rather than a context-augmented self-teacher with
continually resampled negatives. \textbf{STaR} \citep{zelikman2022star}, without rationalization,
iterates self-sampling, verifier filtering,
and self-training on the verified samples of the student, with no
teacher call and no teacher-generated initialization examples. The
agent distiller of \citet{kang2025distilling} reduces, in this
harness, to SFT on the same pool, since its retrieval and
code-tool components are fixed by the benchmark harness for all
methods, and it is therefore not listed as a separate row.

\paragraph{Sealed-pool baseline runs.}
Under the unified budget every baseline draws its evidence from the
same sealed candidate pool as RTD (the pools of the current pair are
collected with GPT-5.6 Luna; the numbers in the rest of this paragraph
describe the earlier DeepSeek pool and are kept for the record), purchased in a frozen random order
(one order shared by the three training runs, which differ only in
training randomness) until the next package would exceed the cap;
purchased failed attempts are charged and yield no positive. Purchased
packages are rendered into training rows as deployment-exact text.
The prompt is the string the official handler sends at evaluation
(the tool list with the language note of the handler, ending at the
assistant turn marker), and the target is what the model must emit
there, the empty non-thinking block followed by the teacher
continuation in native tool-call form. The rendering used by RTD for
the same package differs only in placing the empty block inside the
prompt; both are recorded in the run manifests. Two other renderings
were tried and rejected at the unified data size because the resulting
students emitted an empty block and stopped: the legacy pseudo-tag
rows with the decoded call style of the leaderboard (their earlier
23-row use had merely been under-trained) and rows with the empty
block masked inside the prompt. On BFCL at $B=13{,}843$ the random
order buys 103 packages over 22 tasks (94 positive rows, 9 charged
failures, $C_m=13{,}776$); among these tasks no cached failed first
turn of the student exists, so the offline PBSD arm has no
preference pair and coincides with SFT on this benchmark,
and the on-policy correction arm coincides with it by the single-turn
identity above. The dDPO arm adds one teacher ranking call per
purchased task, charged to $C_m$. The offline preference variant of
PBSD (teacher positives against the failed first turn of the student,
trained jointly with the positive NLL) scored $4.2_{\pm7.2}$ on
AppWorld and $28.8_{\pm4.0}$ on BFCL~v4 under the same protocol and is
not listed in the main table, which keeps the agent adaptation only. STaR trains on the verified samples
of the initial student and charges no teacher token.

\section{Limitations}\label{app:limitations}
The acquisition arms are single-seed exploration runs on one hardware
class, and the main-table rows are three training runs; all
statements about the mechanism rest on paired controls at frozen
checkpoints rather than on repeated end-to-end runs. The experiments
are sealed-pool replays. The candidate pool, responses, and recorded
costs are frozen in advance, so purchase decisions are real but the
costs are recorded rather than incurred online, and estimated costs
are labelled as such. The return gradient is estimated from a finite
rollout batch and reused between decision windows; the feedback age
is reported, and the resulting staleness bias is not corrected. The
lower bound of Section~\ref{sec:theory-mech} assumes local smoothness
along the update path with an unknown constant, so the solver is a
local surrogate rather than a certified maximization. Certification on
the held-out split is plumbing only in this version; no bootstrap
bounds are reported.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
All experiments run from configuration files with sha256-stable splits;
every result row in this paper maps to a line in the experiment log
and a commit. Code, logs, and the amendment history will be released.

\section{Additional analysis}\label{app:analysis}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:target}.}
(i) Each source contributes non-negative masses $(1-a_{ij})/m$ and
$a_{ij}/m$ that sum to $1/m$, so $\hat q\ge0$ and $\sum\hat q=1$;
merging repeated behaviors adds masses and preserves both. A
permutation $\Pi$ of the sources with their shares maps $U\to U\Pi$,
$K\to\Pi^{\top}K\Pi$, $x\to\Pi^{\top}x$ and leaves $\hat q$, both
estimators, $Ua$, the objective, and the feasible box unchanged. (ii)
Expanding $\sum_y\hat q(y)\nabla\ell_\theta(y)$ with $\hat q$ frozen
gives $g^{\mathrm{raw}}$ for either loss normalization. The difference
$g^{\mathrm{CV}}-g^{\mathrm{raw}}=G^{\mathrm{soft}}-\bar G$ contains no
share, so its expectation over the sources and the selection process
is $\mathbb{E}[G^{\mathrm{soft}}-\bar G]$, which is zero whenever the
soft term matches the hard term in expectation; no independence
between the shares and the samples is needed, and nothing is claimed
conditionally on the realized samples. (iii) For total-token
normalization, reaching prefix $t$ is measurable with respect to the
history, so $\mathbb{E}[s_t-g_t(Y_t)\mid h_t]=0$ prefix by prefix and
$\mathbb{E}[S_{\Sigma}-G_{\Sigma}]=0$; at $\pi_\theta=p_t$ every
$s_t$ is the gradient of a minimized per-prefix divergence and
vanishes. For per-sequence normalization the length-corrected
estimator differs from the hard gradient by
$c\,(S_{\Sigma}-G_{\Sigma})$ with $c$ fixed before sampling, hence by
a zero-mean term. The impossibility statement is the following
example. A root with probability $\tfrac12$ of ending at length one
and $\tfrac12$ of continuing to a forced second token, evaluated at the
snapshot, has a length-normalized hard gradient whose root coordinate
is $+\tfrac12$ or $-\tfrac14$ with equal probability, with expectation
$\tfrac18$; any estimator that matches this expectation cannot vanish
on both samples, while the naive prefix term divided by $L$ vanishes
and is therefore biased.

\paragraph{Counterexample for the earlier weighted form.}
Two actions $A$ and $B$ with snapshot probability $\tfrac12$ each,
loss gradients $-\tfrac12$ and $+\tfrac12$, teacher text $A$, and
$G^{\mathrm{soft}}=0$. Let the selector set $\alpha=1$, $d=0$ when the
first source is $B$ and $\alpha=0$ otherwise. Enumerating the four
source pairs gives $\mathbb{E}\,g^{\mathrm{raw}}=\mathbb{E}\,g^{\mathrm{CV}}=-\tfrac38$
while the earlier form
$(1-\alpha)G^{\mathrm{soft}}+\alpha G^T-\tfrac{d}{2}(G_1-G_2)$ gives
$-\tfrac14$; its difference from the raw gradient is
$(1-\alpha)(G^{\mathrm{soft}}-\bar G)$, which carries the adaptive
multiplier. The same example with both sources equal to $B$ and
$a=(1,1)$ gives $g^{\mathrm{CV}}=-1$, outside the range of gradients
of any non-negative mixture of the two actions, which is why a single
batch of the control-variate estimator is not read as the gradient of
a non-negative target.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:affine}.}
Substituting $g_i^{\mathrm{CV}}$ into the frozen linear step
$\theta-\eta P\sum_i w_ig_i$ gives
$\theta-\eta P\sum_i w_iG_i^{\mathrm{soft}}
-\sum_{ij}a_{ij}\,\tfrac{\eta w_i}{m}P(G_i^T-G_{ij})=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+Ua$.
The degeneracies follow from the column definition. The displacement
from the reference is $U(a-a^{\mathrm{ref}})=Ux$.

\paragraph{Convexity, optimality, and the zero condition.}
For $\varepsilon\ge0$ an optimal solution of the lifted program has
$t=|x|$, so it is equivalent to the original problem; $H\succeq0$
gives $K=U^{\top}HU\succeq0$, so the program is convex and the
Karush--Kuhn--Tucker conditions, primal feasibility, stationarity, and
complementary slackness, certify a global optimum of the local
problem. Stationarity at an interior coordinate $\ell$ with $x_\ell=0$
reads $|\hat h^{\top}b_\ell-\lambda\sum_{k\ne\ell}K_{\ell k}x_k|\le\varepsilon_\ell$,
which involves the other coordinates. Instance for the cross terms:
$K=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$,
$U^{\top}\hat h=(0.8,0)$, $\varepsilon=(0,0.1)$, $a^{\mathrm{ref}}=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$,
$\lambda=1$ has the optimum $x=(0.5,-0.2)$, so the second coordinate
moves although its own response is within its uncertainty. Instance
for the scalar restriction: $b_1=(1,1)$, $b_2=(1,-1)$, $\hat h=(0,1)$,
$H=I$, $\lambda=1$, $\varepsilon=0$, $a^{\mathrm{ref}}=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$
gives $F=0$ under $x_1=x_2$ and $F=\tfrac12$ at $x=(\tfrac12,-\tfrac12)$,
which hands one behavior fully to the teacher and keeps the other. A
general sufficient condition is that some feasible difference of
source directions lies in the null space of a retained response and
aligns positively with $h$ while the scalar family contains no such
direction; the comparison class is the scalar restriction on the same
batch and estimator.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:gain}.}
The lower smoothness bound gives
$J(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}+Ux)-J(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})\ge h^{\top}Ux-\tfrac{L_H}{2}\|Ux\|_H^2$.
With $\lambda\ge L_H$ the quadratic term is dominated by
$\tfrac{\lambda}{2}x^{\top}Kx$, and the simultaneous error control
gives $h^{\top}Ux\ge\hat h^{\top}Ux-\sum_\ell\varepsilon_\ell|x_\ell|$;
together these yield $F(x)$. Simultaneity is what allows the bound to
be applied to an $x$ chosen after seeing $\hat h$; per-coordinate
empirical standard errors do not provide it. For a realized
displacement $Ux+e$, the same expansion loses
$(M+L_H\|Ux\|_H)\rho+L_H\rho^2/2$, and an approximate solver loses its
suboptimality $\delta$. Alignment errors not covered by $\varepsilon$
and errors in $K$ enter as additional $\zeta^{\top}|x|$ and
$\lambda\|E_K\|_2\|x\|^2/2$ terms, which must not be counted twice
with $e$.

\paragraph{Value of evidence and nesting.}
With the base update, the old weights, the reference, and $\hat h$
fixed, appending directions with zero shares and a zero-share teacher
version recovers the old objective and the old parameter increment
exactly, up to the recorded numerical repair of $K$; the old feasible
solutions embed in the new problem, so $F^{\star}$ does not decrease
and $A(Q)\ge0$, with equality exactly when the new directions receive
zero use at the optimum. Forcing a new retention term for an added
state would change the base update even at zero share and break this
nesting, so candidate slots and their free retention are fixed before
purchase and only the teacher directions are opened by a purchase.

\paragraph{Budgeted acquisition.}
The ledger invariant, reserved plus settled cost at or below the
authorization, holds at every event because a package is reserved at
its public cap before its response is revealed and the difference
between cap and recorded cost is released only after settlement; the
batch selection under $\sum_q\hat c(q)\le B_t$ therefore never leads
to a purchase that exceeds the authorization, and a package that the
ledger cannot afford at its cap is dropped with a journaled reason
rather than substituted.
