
\section{Additional methodology details}\label{app:method}

This section provides the implementation details omitted from
Section~\ref{sec:method}: the complete procedure of one decision
window, the exposure and ledger conventions, the gate, the estimator
components, the source-selection solver, the acquisition surrogate
with its posterior, and the paired controls used to isolate the
mechanism.

\paragraph{Complete procedure.}
Training runs in budget rounds of twelve commit steps; steps 1, 4, 7,
and 10 are decision windows. A decision window executes, in this
order: (i) select and purchase up to $K$ packages with the historical
posterior under the remaining authorization, revealing and settling
each at its recorded cost through the ledger; (ii) fix the evidence
of the step, the exposure weights $w_i$, and the injections
$\alpha_i$, and draw two fresh source actions per unit from the frozen
snapshot of the round; (iii) build the virtual reference $\theta_S(0)$
on this batch; (iv) estimate the return gradient at $\theta_S(0)$ with
temperature-one rollouts on the feedback fold and solve for $d^\star$;
(v) commit $\theta_S(d^\star)$ on the same batch, estimate the return
at $\theta_S(d^\star)$, and update the gate in a blocked step with
$d^\star$ fixed. The acquisition reference (the old-evidence virtual
update used for surrogate labels) and the selection reference
$\theta_S(0)$ are separate rollout batches with separate compute
accounting and are never substituted for one another. Non-decision
steps sample units from the accumulated pool under the frozen rules,
reuse the latest return gradient, recompute the directions $v_i$ and
the errors $\hat\varepsilon_i$ along them from stored per-trajectory
contributions, and journal the feedback age.

\paragraph{Exposure and ledger conventions.}
One exposure unit is the teacher record of one state plus two source
actions; each commit step uses $E=40$ units (eighty actions), at most
$K=20$ of them from new packages, in micro-batches of four states and
ten micro-batches per commit. Before any purchase the units are
reference-pool states without teacher evidence, for which
$\alpha_i=0$. The ledger keeps reserved plus settled cost at or below
the authorization; a package is reserved at its public cap, revealed,
settled at its recorded cost, and the remainder released; the same
package is paid once; a failed completion is charged. The budget
denominator is the recorded content cost of the sealed pool, and the
authorizations at the ends of the two rounds are ten and twenty-five
percent of it. The support tasks are split into two folds that rotate
between rounds; the inner fold supplies source states and candidates,
and the feedback fold supplies returns only and never enters a
gradient, the preconditioner, or any normalization.

\paragraph{Gate.}
$\alpha(s)=\sigma(\phi\cdot\chi(s))$ uses state-only features (a fixed
projection of the hidden representation of the state, the prompt
length, and the turn index); it excludes the length, likelihood, or
projection of the sampled actions and excludes benchmark categories
and error types. The control arm uses the constant $\alpha=0.5$. The
gate parameters are updated once per decision window from the
post-commit return with $d^\star$ fixed.

\paragraph{Estimator components.}
For a unit with prefixes $Y_1,Y_2$, the soft source gradient at each
sampled prefix position is the gradient of the full-vocabulary
KL between the frozen snapshot $p_t$ and the current student, averaged
over the two prefixes; $G_T$ is the gradient of the log-likelihood of
the teacher text; $G_1,G_2$ are the log-likelihood gradients of the
two sampled actions, with the sampled end-of-sequence token included
and no token appended at a length cap. Generation-time per-token
log-probabilities are checked against teacher-forced rescoring under a
fixed tolerance, so that a sample whose recorded scores disagree with
the model is rejected before it enters any gradient.

\paragraph{Source-selection solver.}
$d^\star$ maximizes $\hat z^{\top}d-\tfrac{\lambda}{2}d^{\top}\tilde K d
-\sum_i\hat\varepsilon_i|d_i|$ over $|d_i|\le\min\{\alpha_i,1-\alpha_i\}$
by projected coordinate ascent with a convergence test, where
$\tilde K=K/s$ with $s$ the mean diagonal of $K$ over units with a
non-zero direction and $\hat z$, $\hat\varepsilon$ scaled by
$1/\sqrt{s}$, so that the solution is invariant to the overall scale
of the directions and $\lambda=1$ means that the normalized curvature
of one unit equals one unit of its linear gain. The estimation error
$\hat\varepsilon_i$ is the leave-one-out standard error of $\hat z_i$
over the feedback rollouts along $v_i$ and is recomputed along the new
directions at every step. The first window of each run uses $d=0$
(warm-up). Journaled per step are $\hat z$, $\hat\varepsilon$, the
interaction statistics of $K$, $d^\star$, the active constraints, and
the feedback age.

\paragraph{Acquisition surrogate and posterior.}
For the evidence $\mathcal{E}$ of the current window the surrogate
value of an owned package is
$\hat F(\mathcal{E})-\hat F(\mathcal{E}\setminus\{q\})$ with
$\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}(d)$ built at the acquisition reference point
and the units of the removed package back-filled with predetermined
old-pool evidence in the same slots and weights; the label is stored
as an acquisition surrogate, distinct from realized paired gains,
which are measured with a different feedback batch and used only to
validate the surrogate. The persistent posterior is a Bayesian linear
model over the public features of the package and the pre-purchase
training context (round and step progress, number of purchased
packages and states, a summary of purchased state projections, mean
and spread of the current $\alpha$, and feedback age) with a separate
cost model over public features only; its mean is shrunk toward zero
unless at least four windows and eight labels show positive
out-of-window predictive power. Nothing in the procedure reads
unpurchased content; the selector sees public identifiers and cost
bounds only, which is enforced by a leak test.

\paragraph{Paired controls.}
On frozen checkpoints and fixed window evidence, the paired
interventions execute two one-step updates from the same start and
evaluate both on a fixed task set and on held-out feedback tasks:
learned $d^\star$ against $d=0$ at the same $\alpha$ (the isolation of
the mechanism); the update with $Y_1$ and $Y_2$ swapped (symmetry:
$d$ flips sign and the update is unchanged); learned $d$ against a
shuffled source pairing that keeps $\alpha$, the teacher targets, and
the multiset of gate values; the joint solver against the independent
solver that drops the off-diagonal of $K$; and the source-estimator
diagnostic (two hard samples, the soft term, eight hard samples,
syntax-filtered hard samples). Each intervention reports the
fixed-set parse-failure rate, greedy repair and damage counts under
equal teacher mass, the held-out return, and the update norm.

\paragraph{Optimization and implementation constants.}
Students train LoRA adapters (rank 16, $\alpha_{\mathrm{LoRA}}=32$,
dropout 0) with AdamW at learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$; the
preconditioner $P$ used for the virtual reference and the directions
$v_i$ is the diagonal preconditioner of the optimizer accumulated on
the inner fold before any purchase. Each commit step uses $E=40$
exposure units, $K\le 20$ new packages, micro-batches of four states,
and ten micro-batches per commit; two source actions per unit at
temperature one, top-$p$ one, resampled at every step; the action cap
is 256 tokens on ALFWorld and the harness cap elsewhere. Return
feedback uses the feedback fold with a fixed number of temperature-one
rollouts per task and leave-one-out standard errors; $\lambda=1$ on
the normalized interaction matrix; warm-up $d=0$ for the first window;
gate features are state-only with a scalar variant
$\alpha=\sigma(\phi_0)$; the control arm fixes $\alpha=0.5$. The
acquisition posterior is shrunk to five percent of its mean until four
windows and eight labels show positive out-of-window predictive power;
public cost caps are 2{,}048 output tokens for demonstration packages
and 512 for generator packages, certified against the sealed pool.
Generation batches at most eight prompts within a padded-token budget;
teacher-forced scoring is unbatched. Experiments ran on a lab node
with mixed NVIDIA GPUs for evaluation and on NVIDIA B200 nodes for the
acquisition arms; all arms of a comparison run on one hardware class.

\paragraph{Benchmark protocols and evaluation metrics.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval-protocol} fixes the evaluation protocol of every
main-table column. \textbf{AppWorld} tasks an agent with day-to-day
objectives across nine simulated applications; we report task goal
completion (TGC), the fraction of tasks whose full evaluation test
suite passes under the official evaluator, on a fixed 40-task subset
of the development split (\texttt{dev40}) with a 24-step cap, and
give the pooled unit-test pass ratio
$\sum_i \mathrm{passed}_i/\sum_i \mathrm{tests}_i$ only as a
diagnostic. The subset is a development result and is labelled as
such. \textbf{BFCL~v4} evaluates function calling across single-turn,
live, multi-turn, memory, web-search, and relevance categories and is
reported as the official overall accuracy over all 5{,}217 entries
under the official generation and evaluation scripts with the
leaderboard default low-temperature sampling ($T=0.001$, served by
vLLM; this is not strict greedy decoding). Trained checkpoints are
always compared with the initial student evaluated on the same
machine and configuration.
\textbf{ALFWorld} tests embodied household instruction following in a
text environment with a ReAct scaffold; we report the success rate on
the 140 \texttt{valid\_seen} tasks with a 40-step cap under greedy
decoding. \textbf{$\tau^2$-bench} places the agent in customer-service
conversations with database-backed tools and a simulated user; with a
metered simulator one episode costs on the order of $10^5$ teacher
tokens, so a single main-table row would exceed the budgets studied
here, and the benchmark is left to future work.

\paragraph{Initial pretrained student row.}
The base row evaluates the released \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} checkpoint
with no adapter and is reported as one reference value per column,
taken on the same machine and configuration as the trained rows it is
compared with. Repeated evaluations of this checkpoint are reported
separately from training variance. On AppWorld three evaluation seeds
give TGC $0/40$, $0/40$, and $1/40$ (pass ratios $0.277$, $0.213$,
$0.250$); on BFCL the standard campaign observed $46.27$, $46.25$, and
$46.06$ on three GPU classes, a spread of $0.21$ points in this run,
which is an observation rather than a bound. The $\pm$ of every
trained row is the standard deviation over its three training runs.

\paragraph{Budget accounting.}
$B$ is a cap on teacher output tokens per benchmark, applied
identically to every teacher-using method; the actual spend of each
method is reported next to its score. Charged items are teacher
demonstrations including discarded failures, teacher ranking outputs
(dDPO), and teacher corrections on student-visited states (on-policy
correction). Student rollouts, environment interaction, verification,
and GPU time are reported separately and are not part of $B$. Where a
method trains from the sealed demonstration pool of the acquisition
experiments, its cost is the recorded output-token cost of the pool
content it uses.

\paragraph{Baseline implementations.}\label{app:baselines}
All baselines train the same LoRA student (rank 16) on the same
support set with identical optimization constants, and differ only in
the training pool and loss. Losses are computed on assistant-generated
spans only; environment observations and prompts are masked. The
branches follow the black-box taxonomy of \citet{xu2024kdsurvey}.
\textbf{SFT} \citep{mukherjee2023orca} minimizes the
token-level NLL on verified teacher trajectories.
\textbf{SAD} (Structured Agent Distillation, \citealp{liu2025structured})
trains on the same pool with reasoning and action spans weighted
separately in the NLL; the teacher-distribution alignment of the
original method is not available from a black-box teacher and is
replaced by hard labels. \textbf{BBOPD}
(black-box on-policy distillation, adapted from \citealp{ye2025bbopd}) lets the rollouts of the student
itself fix the visited states, queries the teacher once per visited
state for the action it would take there, and trains the resulting
(state, teacher action) pairs by NLL; this keeps the on-policy state
distribution of black-box on-policy distillation but not its
discriminator or adversarial objective, and on a single-turn benchmark
the visited state is the query itself, so the pool coincides with the
demonstration pool. \textbf{dDPO} (distilled DPO,
\citealp{tunstall2023zephyr}) runs an SFT stage followed by
DPO whose preference pairs are the samples of the student ranked best
and worst by the teacher; the frozen initial student is the DPO
reference model, and the ranking outputs are charged to $B$.
\textbf{PBSD (offline pref.)} (an offline variant of
\citealp{pbsd2026}) pairs teacher positives with the failed first turn
of the student as the rejected side and trains jointly with the
positive NLL; unlike PBSD it uses a static external teacher and a
fixed negative rather than a context-augmented self-teacher with
continually resampled negatives. \textbf{STaR} \citep{zelikman2022star}, without rationalization,
iterates self-sampling, verifier filtering,
and self-training on the verified samples of the student, with no
teacher call and no teacher-generated initialization examples. The
agent distiller of \citet{kang2025distilling} reduces, in this
harness, to SFT on the same pool, since its retrieval and
code-tool components are fixed by the benchmark harness for all
methods, and it is therefore not listed as a separate row.

\paragraph{Sealed-pool baseline runs.}
Under the unified budget every baseline draws its evidence from the
same sealed candidate pool as RTD, purchased in a frozen random order
(one order shared by the three training runs, which differ only in
training randomness) until the next package would exceed the cap;
purchased failed attempts are charged and yield no positive. Purchased
packages are rendered into training rows as deployment-exact text.
The prompt is the string the official handler sends at evaluation
(the tool list with the language note of the handler, ending at the
assistant turn marker), and the target is what the model must emit
there, the empty non-thinking block followed by the teacher
continuation in native tool-call form. The rendering used by RTD for
the same package differs only in placing the empty block inside the
prompt; both are recorded in the run manifests. Two other renderings
were tried and rejected at the unified data size because the resulting
students emitted an empty block and stopped: the legacy pseudo-tag
rows with the decoded call style of the leaderboard (their earlier
23-row use had merely been under-trained) and rows with the empty
block masked inside the prompt. On BFCL at $B=13{,}843$ the random
order buys 103 packages over 22 tasks (94 positive rows, 9 charged
failures, $C_m=13{,}776$); among these tasks no cached failed first
turn of the student exists, so the offline PBSD arm has no
preference pair and coincides with SFT on this benchmark,
and the on-policy correction arm coincides with it by the single-turn
identity above. The dDPO arm adds one teacher ranking call per
purchased task, charged to $C_m$. STaR trains on the verified samples
of the initial student and charges no teacher token.

\paragraph{AppWorld failure mode of the imitation arms.}
On AppWorld the student column sits near the floor, and the reason is
specific. In the sealed DeepSeek pool 39\% of the purchased assistant
turns are documentation lookups (\texttt{apis.api\_docs.*}), the phase
in which the teacher reads the API surface before acting. Several
trained runs imitate exactly this phase. At every step they emit a
well-formed code block that queries the documentation of one app,
receive the same output, and repeat it until the 24-step cap, so the
environment state never changes and only the tests that hold on an
untouched state pass. Runs that escape the loop act on the environment
and pass more tests but rarely finish a task within the cap. We
therefore report the pooled test pass ratio next to the task goal
completion for this column as a diagnostic; the two agree on ordering.

\paragraph{Version 1.0 arms (record).}
Before the mechanism of Section~\ref{sec:distill} was fixed, an earlier
design (v1.0) used a fixed injection $\alpha=0.5$ with no return-chosen
replacement (R0, random acquisition), the full acquisition and selection
loop (R1), and a learned scalar gate in place of the state injection
(R1s), all under sealed replay of the same pool over three budget
rounds. On BFCL v4 the three arms finished at 46.75 (R0, seven
packages, 686 tokens), 45.31 (R1, six packages, 571 tokens, evaluated
on a different device whose base reference is 46.27) and 46.53 (R1s,
six packages, 523 tokens) against a base of 46.06 on the same device
as R0 and R1s. On ALFWorld valid\_seen (140 tasks, greedy) they
finished at 52.86 (R0, five packages, 870 tokens), 43.57 (R1, seven
packages, 2,059 tokens) and 53.57 (R1s, seven packages, 2,059 tokens)
against a base of 7.14. No v1.0 arm separates from its control beyond
evaluation noise on BFCL, and on ALFWorld the full loop falls below
the control at the third round. These runs are the reason the
replacement decision was moved from the teacher text to the return of
the student itself in v1.1, and they are not part of the main table.

\paragraph{Component-study selection and data-generation baselines.}
Beyond the main-table branches, the component studies compare against
selection baselines spanning classical and current families, namely
random subsets, embedding retrieval, LESS \citep{xia2024less}, GIST
\citep{gist2026}, TRIM \citep{trim2025}, ICONS \citep{icons2025}, and
the capacity-aligned selection of SmartAD \citep{tang2026smartad}, and
against the data-generation baselines Self-Instruct
\citep{wang2023selfinstruct}, Evol-Instruct \citep{xu2024wizardlm},
and LLM2LLM \citep{lee2024llm2llm}.

\section{Limitations}\label{app:limitations}
The acquisition arms are single-seed exploration runs on one hardware
class, and the main-table rows are three training runs; all
statements about the mechanism rest on paired controls at frozen
checkpoints rather than on repeated end-to-end runs. The experiments
are sealed-pool replays. The candidate pool, responses, and recorded
costs are frozen in advance, so purchase decisions are real but the
costs are recorded rather than incurred online, and estimated costs
are labelled as such. The return gradient is estimated from a finite
rollout batch and reused between decision windows; the feedback age
is reported, and the resulting staleness bias is not corrected. The
lower bound of Section~\ref{sec:theory-mech} assumes local smoothness
along the update path with an unknown constant, so the solver is a
local surrogate rather than a certified maximization. Certification on
the held-out split is plumbing only in this version; no bootstrap
bounds are reported.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
All experiments run from configuration files with sha256-stable splits;
every result row in this paper maps to a line in the experiment log
and a commit. Code, logs, and the amendment history will be released.

\section{Additional analysis}\label{app:analysis}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer}.}
(i) The three coefficients $\tfrac{1-\alpha-d}{2}$,
$\tfrac{1-\alpha+d}{2}$, and $\alpha$ are non-negative for
$|d|\le\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}$ and sum to one, and the teacher
component has coefficient $\alpha$ independently of $d$. (ii) The
cross-entropy to $q$ decomposes into the three components with the
same coefficients; its gradient in expectation over
$Y_1,Y_2\sim p_t$ equals $(1-\alpha)\,\mathbb{E}[\bar G_{\mathrm{soft}}]
+\alpha G_T-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[d\,(G_1-G_2)]$ because the
$(1-\alpha)$ part of the hard-sample terms, whose coefficient does
not depend on the samples, can be replaced by the full-vocabulary
soft term with the same expectation, while the $d$ part keeps the
sampled actions since $d$ depends on them; the estimator $\hat g_d$
is the corresponding single-sample form, and its expectation matches
term by term. At $\pi_\theta=p_t$ the per-position KL is minimized,
so its gradient is zero. (iii) Substituting $G_k=u(s)+e_k$ gives
$G_1-G_2=e_1-e_2$.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:lowerbound}.}
$L_H$-smoothness of $J$ along the segment from $\theta_S(0)$ to
$\theta_S(d)$ in the $H$-norm gives
$J(\theta_S(d))\ge J(\theta_S(0))+\nabla J(\theta_S(0))^{\top}(d\,v)
-\tfrac{L_H}{2}\|d\,v\|_H^2$, which is the stated bound with
$z=\nabla J(\theta_S(0))^{\top}v$. For $z\ne 0$ the right-hand side
is positive for $d$ of the sign of $z$ with $|d|<2|z|/(L_H\|v\|_H^2)$,
and the box $|d|\le\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}$ contains such $d$ whenever
$0<\alpha<1$. Replacing $z$ by an estimate with $|\hat z-z|\le\varepsilon$
lowers the bound by at most $\varepsilon|d|$.

\paragraph{Joint selection over several units.}
With $\Delta\theta=\sum_i d_i v_i$ the same argument yields
$J(\theta_S(d))-J(\theta_S(0))\ge z^{\top}d-\tfrac{L_H}{2}d^{\top}Kd$
with $K_{ij}=v_i^{\top}Hv_j$; the off-diagonal terms are the cost of
two units pushing the update in a shared direction. The interior zero
condition of the surrogate for coordinate $i$ is
$|\hat z_i-\lambda\sum_{j\ne i}\tilde K_{ij}d_j|\le\hat\varepsilon_i$,
which involves the other coordinates; a unit with a small direct
response can therefore be used to offset the cost of other directions,
and no coordinate is discarded before the joint problem is solved.

\paragraph{Budgeted acquisition.}
The ledger invariant, reserved plus settled cost at or below the
authorization, holds at every event because a package is reserved at
its public cap before its response is revealed and the difference
between cap and recorded cost is released only after settlement; the
batch selection under $\sum_q\hat c(q)\le B_t$ therefore never leads
to a purchase that exceeds the authorization, and a package that the
ledger cannot afford at its cap is dropped with a journaled reason
rather than substituted.
