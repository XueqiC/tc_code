
\section{Additional methodology details}\label{app:method}

This section provides implementation details omitted from
Section~\ref{sec:method}, including the complete training procedure,
gradient representation, acquisition estimators, reference-task
interference measurement, guidance construction, importance weighting,
the preference construction we tested and removed, and task-boundary
calibration.

\paragraph{Complete training algorithm.}
The procedure begins by randomly partitioning
$\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_d\cup\mathcal{S}_c$ with
$\mathcal{S}_d\cap\mathcal{S}_c=\emptyset$, reserving $\mathcal{S}_c$
exclusively for final calibration, constructing the initial gradient
representation and dictionary from admissible training-side data, and
estimating the task-demand vector $r$ from $\mathcal{S}_d$. Each round
then proceeds in four phases. Acquisition computes the remaining
deficits $\delta_{r,j}=(r_j-s_{r,j})_+$, estimates coverage
$C_r(x)$, unlock probability $\hat\rho_r(x)$, cost $\hat c(x)$, and
interference $I_r(x)$ for each feasible candidate, and greedily
acquires the highest-utility candidates while the budget lasts and
$\max_x U_r(x)>0$; every delivered demonstration is verified and
converted into a context $h(d_x)$. Collection samples $K$ unguided
trajectories per query, records each trajectory, verifier outcome,
provenance, and behavior-policy log-likelihood, and activates an
acquired context only for queries whose unguided attempts all fail.
Optimization computes clipped importance weights for guided
trajectories, forms the verifier-weighted positive objective from all
collected student trajectories, and updates the student with
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}$; failed trajectories enter
no loss term. Re-estimation
re-evaluates unguided capability, updates supply $s_{r+1}$, unlock and
interference estimates, and acquisition utilities, and stops when the
unguided convergence criterion holds, the budget is exhausted, or all
remaining candidates have non-positive utility. Calibration then
freezes the student and every component of the routing score before
touching $\mathcal{S}_c$.

\paragraph{Gradient features and coupled dictionary.}
Gradient features are computed with respect to the same restricted
parameter subset used for specialization; raw gradients are
transformed by the Adam preconditioner before a fixed random
projection $P\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times D}$ with $p\ll D$ is applied. When
both prompt-side and outcome-side gradients are available, we fit a
coupled sparse dictionary with shared latent coefficients; missing
outcome views are handled through an explicit availability mask rather
than zero imputation.

\paragraph{Demand, supply, and saturation.}
Per-atom demand aggregates query-level normalized activations over
$\mathcal{S}_d$, so that a few high-magnitude queries cannot dominate
the estimate. A verified student trajectory contributes supply
according to its projected gradient representation, and the positive
part $(r_j-s_{r,j})_+$ gives acquisition diminishing returns along
already-covered directions.

\paragraph{Estimating the unlock probability.}
Before any guided observations exist, all candidates share a common
prior, so initial acquisition is driven by demand coverage, predicted
cost, and interference risk. Afterwards $\rho_r$ is updated from
empirical guided success statistics with smoothing across nearby task
representations. Teacher success is never used as an estimate of
student success under guidance.

\paragraph{Reference-task interference.}
The penalty $I_r(x)$ is estimated from a fixed set of free
reference-task gradients through the reference coupling statistic
$\kappa^r(x)$, which distinguishes task-aligned transfer,
approximately orthogonal interaction, and antagonistic coupling;
$I_r(x)$ is a monotone function of the antagonistic component.
Section~\ref{sec:rq5} evaluates whether these geometric regimes
predict measured reference-task interference after specialization.

\paragraph{Guidance construction.}
A purchased demonstration is transformed into a context block and
inserted into the student's generation context according to the
environment interface. Teacher-generated actions are never copied into
the optimization target, and the context is removed when computing the
deployment-policy likelihood $\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)$.

\paragraph{Importance weighting and support.}
Every collected trajectory stores its behavior-policy log-likelihood
$\log\mu_r(\tau\mid x)$. The trajectory-level log ratio
$\log w_\theta=\log\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)-\log\mu_r(\tau\mid x)$ is
computed in log space and clipped at $c$. The exact identity assumes
absolute continuity, which contextual guidance does not guarantee,
since a guidance context can make some unguided-reachable trajectories
unreachable under the behavior policy; clipping controls variance on
observed trajectories but cannot recover missing support. We report
the empirical ratio distribution and compare no correction, unclipped
correction where feasible, and clipped correction.

\paragraph{Preference construction tested and removed.}
An earlier version of the objective added a reference-free odds-ratio
preference term \citep{hong2024orpo} on success-failure pairs sharing
the environment state before their first action divergence $t^\star$,
with tokens before $t^\star$ masked and pairs admitted through a
length-normalized NLL gate tracked by an exponential moving average.
Auditing the admitted pairs showed why this term degrades the method.
Sampled trajectories diverge in surface form almost immediately, so
the shared prefix averages five percent of the turn and the mask
removes essentially nothing. The punished segment is then nearly the
whole failed turn, of which ninety-six percent of tokens also occur
in verified successful turns of other tasks and seventy-two percent
occur in the paired chosen turn itself, so the negative gradient
lands mostly on the shared task dialect that the positive objective
is reinforcing. The admission gate passed almost every pair in every
epoch, making the damage systematic rather than incidental.
Empirically the term halves the rate of fully completed tasks and
widens seed variance while providing no mean improvement. The final
method therefore trains on $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}$ alone, and
Section~\ref{sec:rq5} reports the paired comparison.

\paragraph{Single-dose positive replay.}
The primary verifier objective is computed from the actual sampled
rollout distribution and does not select trajectories by length. When
persistent positive replay is used, at most one verified anchor per
query per collection stage is retained, sampled uniformly from
eligible successful trajectories.

\paragraph{Task-boundary calibration.}
After all training decisions are complete, the student, dictionary,
and score construction are frozen. The deployment score is
$R(x)=\|g_p(x)-D_p z(x)\|_2$; calibration residuals on
$\mathcal{S}_c$ give the finite-sample corrected quantile
$\gamma_\alpha=R_{(k)}$ with $k=\lceil(m{+}1)(1{-}\alpha)\rceil$, and
the routing rule sends $R(x)\le\gamma_\alpha$ to the specialist and
rejects or escalates otherwise. Because the score construction is
frozen before $\mathcal{S}_c$ is accessed, the guarantee is the
standard split-conformal marginal guarantee under exchangeability and
should not be read as a conditional or out-of-distribution detection
guarantee.

\paragraph{Controlled suite.} Five domains built from public datasets:
GSM8K in two action spaces (execution-verified Python and original
chain-of-thought, an identical-query hard pair), DS-1000 Pandas
\citep{lai2023ds1000}, Spider text-to-SQL, and Alpaca instructions
\citep{taori2023alpaca} as the out-of-domain pool (sha256-stable splits).
Teacher trajectories carry execution-based quality gates (95--96\%
verified on GSM8K-code). Secondary agent environments for breadth:
BFCL~v4 function calling, $\tau^2$-bench \citep{yao2024taubench},
ALFWorld \citep{shridhar2021alfworld}, and WebShop \citep{yao2022webshop},
matching environments used by agent-distillation baselines
\citep{liu2025structured, kang2025distilling}.
\paragraph{Additional baselines.} Distillation-side: vanilla trajectory
SFT, capacity-aligned selection \citep{tang2026smartad}, segment-weighted
losses \citep{liu2025structured}. Boundary-side: query- and
trajectory-embedding subspaces under the same conformal pipeline, and
activation-probe rejection \citep{li2026scope}.
\paragraph{Evaluation details.} Per-environment metrics follow each
benchmark's convention (execution accuracy for GSM8K-style tasks, task
success for AppWorld/ALFWorld, score for WebShop, tool-call accuracy for
BFCL, pass\^{}k-style success for $\tau^2$-bench). Decoding is greedy
(pass@1) with a 320-token cap. Behavioral leakage channels (action-space
adoption, format bleed, refusal rates) accompany the residue numbers.
Certificates follow conformal risk practice \citep{vovk2005algorithmic,
gibbs2025conditional}.
\paragraph{Optimization and implementation constants.}
AdamW, batch size 1 with gradient accumulation 8, learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$, three epochs per optimization phase,
importance-ratio clip $c=1$; the removed preference variant used
weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{pref}}=0.25$ and EMA decay $0.99$ for its
admission gate. Gradient
features use LoRA rank 16 with a fixed random projection to 64
dimensions per module; the Adam preconditioner for the probe gradients
is accumulated on the support queries themselves before any purchase,
so no feature depends on purchased data. The coupled dictionary uses
mini-batch sparse coding with $\lambda_z=0.05$ and atom count
$\min(64,n/2)$ for $n$ available examples, refit as purchases and
verified trajectories accumulate. Collection uses $K=4$ unguided and
$K_g=4$ guided rollouts per query at temperature $0.7$. Acquisition
rounds are bounded by a maximum of $R=2$ in the current experiments;
unused budget carries forward and need not be spent. Single-dose
positive replay retains at most one verified anchor per query per
collection stage, sampled uniformly among eligible successes.
Conformal calibration uses the ten held-out calibration queries.
Experiments ran on one lab GPU node and a SLURM cluster of NVIDIA
B200 accelerators.


\paragraph{Benchmark protocols and evaluation metrics.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval-protocol} fixes the evaluation protocol of every
main-table column. \textbf{AppWorld} tasks an agent with day-to-day
objectives across nine simulated applications; we report task goal
completion (TGC), the fraction of tasks whose full evaluation test
suite passes under the official evaluator, on a fixed 40-task subset
of the development split (\texttt{dev40}) with a 24-step cap, and
give the pooled unit-test pass ratio
$\sum_i \mathrm{passed}_i/\sum_i \mathrm{tests}_i$ only as a
diagnostic. The subset is a development result and is labelled as
such. \textbf{BFCL~v4} evaluates function calling across single-turn,
live, multi-turn, memory, web-search, and relevance categories and is
reported as the official overall accuracy over all 5{,}217 entries
under the official generation and evaluation scripts with the
leaderboard default temperature of $0.001$ (served by vLLM, which
treats this as near-argmax sampling rather than strict greedy
decoding; repeated evaluations of one checkpoint differ by at most
$0.2$ points and hardware classes by at most $0.3$).
\textbf{ALFWorld} tests embodied household instruction following in a
text environment with a ReAct scaffold; we report the success rate on
the 140 \texttt{valid\_seen} tasks with a 40-step cap under greedy
decoding. \textbf{$\tau^2$-bench} places the agent in customer-service
conversations with database-backed tools and a simulated user; with a
metered simulator one episode costs on the order of $10^5$ teacher
tokens, so a single main-table row would exceed the budgets studied
here, and the benchmark is left to future work.

\paragraph{Initial pretrained student row.}
The base row evaluates the released \texttt{Qwen3.5-4B} checkpoint
with no adapter. It is reported as one reference value per column; the
spread across repeated evaluations of the same checkpoint
(AppWorld: three evaluation seeds give TGC $0/40$, $0/40$, $1/40$ and
pass ratios $0.277/0.213/0.250$; BFCL: $46.27$, $46.25$, and $46.06$
on three GPU classes) is evaluation noise and is never mixed with the
training-seed standard deviations of the trained rows.

\paragraph{Budget accounting.}
$B$ is a cap on teacher output tokens per benchmark, applied
identically to every teacher-using method; the actual spend of each
method is reported next to its score. Charged items are teacher
demonstrations including discarded failures, teacher ranking outputs
(dDPO), and teacher corrections on student-visited states (on-policy
correction). Student rollouts, environment interaction, verification,
and GPU time are reported separately and are not part of $B$. Where a
method trains from the sealed demonstration pool of the acquisition
experiments, its cost is the recorded output-token cost of the pool
content it uses.

\paragraph{Baseline implementations.}\label{app:baselines}
All baselines train the same LoRA student (rank 16) on the same
support set with identical optimization constants, and differ only in
the training pool and loss. Losses are computed on assistant-generated
spans only; environment observations and prompts are masked. The
branches follow the black-box taxonomy of \citet{xu2024kdsurvey}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Trajectory SFT} \citep{mukherjee2023orca}: token-level
NLL on verified teacher trajectories.
\item \textbf{Span-weighted SFT} (SAD adaptation,
\citealp{liu2025structured}): the same pool with reasoning and action
spans weighted separately in the NLL; the original method's
teacher-distribution alignment is not available from a black-box
teacher and is replaced by hard labels.
\item \textbf{On-policy teacher correction + SFT} (adapted from
\citealp{ye2025bbopd}): the student's own rollouts fix the visited
states; the teacher is queried once per visited state for the action
it would take there, and the (state, teacher action) pairs are trained
by NLL. This keeps the on-policy state distribution of black-box
on-policy distillation but not its discriminator or adversarial
objective. On a single-turn benchmark the visited state is the query
itself and the pool coincides with the demonstration pool.
\item \textbf{SFT + teacher-ranked DPO} (dDPO, \citealp{tunstall2023zephyr}):
a Trajectory-SFT stage followed by DPO whose preference pairs are the
student's own samples ranked best/worst by the teacher; the frozen
initial student is the DPO reference model, and the ranking outputs
are charged to $B$.
\item \textbf{Offline teacher--student preference training}
(PBSD-inspired, \citealp{pbsd2026}): teacher positives paired with the
student's own failed first turn as the rejected side, trained jointly
with the positive NLL; unlike PBSD it uses a static external teacher
and a fixed negative rather than a context-augmented self-teacher with
continually resampled negatives.
\item \textbf{STaR without rationalization} \citep{zelikman2022star}:
iterated self-sampling, verifier filtering, and self-training on the
student's own verified samples; no teacher call and no
teacher-generated initialization examples.
\end{itemize}
The agent distiller of \citet{kang2025distilling} reduces, in this
harness, to Trajectory SFT on the same pool (its retrieval and
code-tool components are fixed by the benchmark harness for all
methods) and is therefore not listed as a separate row.

\paragraph{Component-study selection and data-generation baselines.}
Beyond the main-table branches, component studies compare against
selection baselines spanning classical and current families, namely
random subsets, embedding retrieval, LESS \citep{xia2024less}, GIST
\citep{gist2026}, TRIM \citep{trim2025}, ICONS \citep{icons2025}, and
SmartAD's capacity-aligned selection \citep{tang2026smartad}, and
against data-generation baselines Self-Instruct
\citep{wang2023selfinstruct}, Evol-Instruct \citep{xu2024wizardlm},
and LLM2LLM \citep{lee2024llm2llm}.

\section{Limitations and pre-registration}\label{app:limitations}
Pilot-scale numbers are single-seed and use verified pseudo-trajectories or
two teachers; sweep-scale results carry seeds and confidence intervals as
they land. The capability-proxy gate governs what may enter the training
loop: geometric readings stay outside it until the pre-registered validation
passes. Suppression claims are behavioral unless paired with unlearning;
base-model prior capability cannot be removed by distillation alone, and our
base/instruct dual track separates what shaping adds from what priors
contribute. Dictionary hyperparameters materially affect atom quality (an
over-sparse setting fails); stability analyses are required before
skill-level claims generalize. \todo{keep synchronized with amendment log.}

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}
All experiments run from configuration files with sha256-stable splits; every
result row in this paper maps to a line in the experiment log and a commit.
Code, logs, and the amendment history will be released.

\section{Additional analysis}\label{app:analysis}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:advantage}.}
The weighted positive objective is
$\sum_{x}a(x)\,\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}[V(x,\tau)\log
\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)]$ up to the collection distribution, and its
gradient is the corresponding weighted sum of the per-task
policy-gradient directions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pg-identity}, which gives
(i). If $\hat p(x)=1$ for all $x$ then $a(x)=0$ for all $x$, the
objective is identically zero, and every parameter setting is a
maximizer, so the procedure returns the initialization, which gives
(ii). If $\hat p(x)\to 0$ uniformly then $a(x)\to 1$ and the
objective converges to the unweighted verifier-filtered objective,
which gives (iii). \qed

\paragraph{Guided collection and importance correction.}
Guided trajectories are drawn from
$\mu_r(\cdot\mid x)=\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x,h(d_x))$ while the
optimized policy is unguided. The importance-sampling identity of
Section~\ref{sec:distill} holds under absolute continuity of
$\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ with respect to $\mu_r(\cdot\mid x)$, which
contextual guidance does not guarantee, and clipping the ratio at $c$
introduces bias in exchange for bounded variance. We therefore treat
the clipped estimator as an approximation whose ratio distribution we
report, and we compare uncorrected, clipped, and where feasible
unclipped variants empirically.

\paragraph{Budgeted acquisition.}
The capped coverage term $\sum_j\min\{\delta_{r,j},\hat s_j(\cdot)\}$
is monotone submodular in the acquired set, so greedy selection under
a knapsack constraint inherits the classical near-optimality
guarantees for that term alone \citep{khuller1999budgeted,
krause2005near}. The deployed utility additionally contains the
unlock probability and the interference penalty, and we do not claim
that the guarantee extends to the penalized product form; greedy
selection there is a heuristic whose value the acquisition ablations
measure.

\paragraph{Geometry preservation under sketching.}
The gradient features compose an adapter sketch with a
Johnson--Lindenstrauss projection \citep{johnson1984extensions}. The
standard guarantee preserves norms and pairwise distances of a finite
point set to multiplicative $(1\pm\varepsilon)$ with logarithmic
dimension dependence, and by polarization the inner products the
method consumes are preserved up to an additive distortion controlled
by the same $\varepsilon$ and the vector norms. Inner products are
the only property that demand estimation, supply accounting, and the
interference statistic consume.

\paragraph{First-order interference prediction.}
For an update $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\sum_{x\in D}
\nabla\ell(x;\theta_t)$, a first-order expansion gives
$\Delta\ell(q)\approx-\eta\sum_{x\in D}
\langle\nabla\ell(q;\theta),\nabla\ell(x;\theta)\rangle$, so predicted
movement of a reference task is the kernel mass the training set
supplies to it. The expansion is exact only in the lazy regime; we
measure its predictive quality rather than assume the regime, and
Section~\ref{sec:rq5} evaluates whether the coupling regimes estimated
from these inner products predict measured reference-task interference
after specialization.

\paragraph{What imitation reproduces.}
Nothing in the maximum-likelihood objective distinguishes task-required
movement from source-specific regularities. We quantify the
distinction with the projection diagnostic $R_{\mathrm{ext}}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:atoms}, and Section~\ref{sec:rq2} tests whether it
predicts the observed degradation ordering across supervision formats.
The diagnostic is a measurement, not an assumption, since the
dictionary provides a common basis rather than an intrinsic separation
of helpful and harmful directions.

\paragraph{Why split-conformal certification is the right tool here.}
The deployment gate must decide, per query, whether the specialized
student may answer or the query falls back to the teacher, under three
constraints inherited from the problem setting. First, the student is
served as a black box at deployment, so the score can only be a
function of its observable output, and split-conformal calibration
requires nothing more than such a score. Second, the few-shot premise
gives one small exchangeable sample $\mathcal{S}_c$ from the in-task
distribution and nothing else, and split-conformal is exactly the
procedure whose finite-sample marginal guarantee consumes only
exchangeability, with no density estimation, no parametric assumption,
and no asymptotics. Third, the guarantee degrades gracefully with
calibration size; with $|\mathcal{S}_c|=n$, realized coverage follows
the known Beta law around the nominal level, so the certificate
remains valid while honestly widening, which matches the budget-first
design of the method. The alternatives all violate one of the
constraints; held-out validation of a fixed threshold gives no
finite-sample guarantee at these sample sizes, and density-ratio or
Bayesian gates require modeling assumptions the black-box setting
cannot check. The certificate is marginal over in-task queries, and we
report out-of-task rejection as an empirical quantity because no
distribution-free guarantee is possible there without assumptions on
the out-of-task law.
