Language-model agents are deployed for narrow jobs that are specified,
in practice, by a few dozen example queries, a task-level success
check, and metered access to a stronger teacher that charges for every
token and exposes only text. We study few-shot agent specialization
under exactly these terms. Distillation from such a teacher faces one
question that imitation cannot answer: a purchased demonstration says
what the teacher would do, but not which of the student's own
behaviors it should replace, and maximum-likelihood imitation replaces
them wholesale. We propose return-targeted distillation. Each
supervised state receives a fixed amount of teacher probability mass
through a legal probability transfer, and the mass is taken from
whichever of two freshly sampled student behaviors the student's own
learning return says should give way, so that the shared component of
the two behaviors cancels and only their difference is decided. The
replacement is the solution of a small joint problem whose
interaction term charges evidence that pushes the update in the same
direction, and the same problem prices every candidate purchase by
the marginal gain it adds to the full update of the evidence already
owned, under a hard ledger that bills failed completions. Under a
common teacher budget cap on agent benchmarks, with the harness fixed
and only the student backbone changing, we compare the method with
implementation-faithful distillation baselines and isolate the
mechanism with paired controls on identical purchased data.
