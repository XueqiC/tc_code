Language-model agents are deployed for narrow jobs that are specified,
in practice, by a few dozen example queries, a task-level success
check, and metered access to a stronger teacher that charges for every
token and exposes only text. We study few-shot agent specialization
under exactly these terms. Distillation from such a teacher faces a
question that imitation cannot answer: a purchased demonstration says
what the teacher would do, but not which of the existing behaviors of
the student it should replace, and maximum-likelihood imitation
replaces all of them at once. We propose return-targeted distillation.
Each supervised state receives a fixed amount of teacher probability
mass through a legal probability transfer, and the mass is taken from
whichever of two freshly sampled student behaviors the learning return
of the student says should give way, so that the component shared by
the two behaviors cancels and only their difference is decided. The
replacement is the solution of a small joint problem whose interaction
term charges evidence that pushes the update in a direction already
taken, and the same problem prices every candidate purchase by the
marginal gain it adds to the full update of the evidence already
owned, under a hard ledger that bills failed completions. Under a
common teacher budget cap on agent benchmarks, with the harness fixed
and only the student backbone changing, we compare the method with
implementation-faithful distillation baselines and isolate the
mechanism with paired controls on identical purchased data.
