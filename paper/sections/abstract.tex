Language-model agents are deployed for narrow jobs that are specified,
in practice, by a few dozen example queries, a task-level success
check, and metered access to a stronger teacher that charges for every
token and exposes only text. We study few-shot agent specialization
under exactly these terms. Distillation from such a teacher faces a
question that imitation cannot answer: a purchased demonstration says
what the teacher would do, but not which of the existing behaviors of
the student it should replace, and maximum-likelihood imitation
replaces all of them at once. We propose return-targeted distillation.
At each supervised state the student first exhibits its own behaviors
by sampling; every sampled behavior receives its own share to hand to
the teacher demonstration, so that the teacher dose and the choice of
what gives way are one decision rather than two. The shares of a whole
training step are chosen by the learning return of the student,
measured after a virtual update, through one convex problem whose
interaction term charges replacements that push the update in shared
directions, and the estimator that installs them keeps the
expectation of the target gradient even though the shares depend on
the same samples. The same problem prices every candidate purchase by
the gain it adds to what the evidence already owned can teach, under a
hard ledger that bills failed completions. Under a common teacher
budget cap on agent benchmarks, with the harness fixed and only the
student backbone changing, we compare the method with
implementation-faithful distillation baselines and isolate the
mechanism with paired controls on identical purchased data.
