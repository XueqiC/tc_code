\begin{abstract}
Language-model agents are deployed for narrow jobs that are specified, in
practice, by nothing more than a few dozen example queries. We ask what it
takes to trust a small distilled agent under so thin a specification, and
reduce the answer to two requirements: the task's boundary must be
determined from the examples alone, and the agent's capability must end
where that boundary ends. We show that both can be read from a single
object. The gradients that teacher demonstrations induce at the student's
initialization decompose, under a sparse dictionary, into \emph{capability
atoms}: recurring, nameable skills that principal subspaces provably
cannot identify. The example queries then become a demand ledger over
atoms, every candidate trace a per-token supply schedule, and distillation
reduces to clearing this market under the token budget. The result is a
certified agent: behaviorally timed training, finite-sample bounds on both
sides of the boundary, and a conformal gate at deployment. Across a
five-domain suite, atom-based selection matches the strongest
influence-based baseline in-task while holding off-task capability at
zero, where that baseline leaks up to $77\%$; certified leakage bounds and
budget-robustness follow at no extra cost.
\end{abstract}
