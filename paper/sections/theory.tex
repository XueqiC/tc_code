\section{Theoretical analysis}\label{sec:theory}
%% One page. Brief intro, then two subsections of comparable length:
%% (i) what the transfer optimizes; (ii) what the acquisition surrogate
%% guarantees and what it does not. Proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}.

We analyze the two components on which the method's claims rest. The
first subsection characterizes the transfer of
Section~\ref{sec:distill}, stating that the teacher mass is invariant
to the replacement decision, that the estimator is unbiased with the
shared behavior component cancelled, and that a small selective
transfer improves the finite-step return whenever the two student
behaviors respond differently. The second subsection states what the
acquisition surrogate of Section~\ref{sec:acquire} does and does not
guarantee. Supporting derivations appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}.

\subsection{What the transfer optimizes}\label{sec:theory-mech}
Fix a supervised state $s$ with injection $\alpha\in[0,1]$ decided
before sampling, two source actions $Y_1,Y_2\sim p_t(\cdot\mid s)$,
and a selection $d=d(s,Y_1,Y_2)$ with $|d|\le\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}$.
Maximum-likelihood training on the teacher text alone follows
$-\nabla_\theta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\nu_T\|\pi_\theta)$ per state, so its
expected update moves the student toward the teacher's behavior
distribution as a whole and cannot distinguish the movement the task
requires from the replacement of behaviors the student already had
right. The target $q$ of Section~\ref{sec:distill} replaces this by a
mixture whose teacher component is fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed teacher mass and unbiased selective
transfer]\label{prop:transfer}
(i) For every legal $d$, $q$ is a probability distribution whose
teacher mass equals $\alpha$; the selection changes only which of
$Y_1,Y_2$ loses mass. (ii) If $\alpha$ and the unit weights $w_i$ are
fixed before $Y_1,Y_2$ are sampled, then
$\hat g_d=(1-\alpha)\bar G_{\mathrm{soft}}+\alpha G_T-\tfrac{d}{2}(G_1-G_2)$
is an unbiased estimate of the gradient of the expected
cross-entropy to $q$, even though $d$ may depend on both samples, and
the soft term vanishes exactly at $\pi_\theta=p_t$. (iii) Writing
$G_k=u(s)+e_k$, the selective term equals $-\tfrac{d}{2}(e_1-e_2)$:
the component $u(s)$ shared by the two behaviors cancels
identically.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}; the finite-action case
is also verified by enumeration in the implementation. Point (ii) is
the reason the injection and the weights are frozen before sampling,
since a weight that depends on the current samples or gradients
introduces a bias term $\mathbb{E}[w_i(G_{\mathrm{hard}}-G_{\mathrm{soft}})]\ne 0$.
Point (iii) is what lets the selection act on the difference between
competing student behaviors without first identifying that difference
across events.

\begin{proposition}[Selective transfer improves the finite-step
return]\label{prop:lowerbound}
Let $\theta_S(0)$ and $\theta_S(d)=\theta_S(0)+d\,v$ be the virtual
reference and selected students built on the same batch, weights,
injections, and source samples, and let
$z=\nabla J(\theta_S(0))^{\top}v$. If $J$ is $L_H$-smooth along the
update path in the $H$-norm, then
$J(\theta_S(d))-J(\theta_S(0))\ge d\,z-\tfrac{L_H}{2}d^2\|v\|_H^2$.
Hence whenever $z\ne 0$ there is a legal $d$ of the sign of $z$ with
strictly positive gain at unchanged teacher mass, and with an
estimate $\hat z$ satisfying $|\hat z-z|\le\varepsilon$ the guaranteed
gain is $d\hat z-\varepsilon|d|-\tfrac{L_H}{2}d^2\|v\|_H^2$.
\end{proposition}

The bound is the origin of the surrogate solved at every step: the
linear term is the estimated response, the $\ell_1$ term is the
estimation error along each direction, and the quadratic term, with
$L_H$ unknown and replaced by a scale-free $\lambda$ on the normalized
interaction matrix $K$, penalizes both the step size and the
redundancy between units. We do not claim monotone improvement; the
return gradient is estimated from rollouts, the smoothness constant is
unknown, and between decision windows the estimate is stale, which the
implementation records as feedback age and does not correct.

\subsection{What the acquisition surrogate guarantees}\label{sec:theory-acq}
On the full evidence set, $\hat F(\mathcal{E})$ coincides with the
selection objective, so the value of an owned package,
$\hat F(\mathcal{E})-\hat F(\mathcal{E}\setminus\{q\})$, is a
leave-one-out difference of the same surrogate evaluated at one
reference point with one return gradient; it counts the teacher
injection the package brings, since $\Delta_{\mathcal{E}}(0)$ does not
vanish when the selection shrinks to zero, and it is comparable across
packages only because the removed package's exposure is back-filled
so that every other weight is unchanged. Two delimitations matter.
First, the surrogate is a first-order-plus-quadratic model of the
finite update; it is validated, not certified, by realized paired
gains measured on a feedback batch different from the one that chose
$d$, and the posterior that maps pre-purchase features to surrogate
values is shrunk toward zero while its out-of-window predictive power
is low. Second, the responses of unpurchased packages are invisible,
so their pairwise interactions cannot be computed; the posterior
predicts marginal gains of purchased packages from public features and
the pre-purchase training context, and nothing in the procedure reads
unpurchased content. The budget itself is enforced by the ledger
invariant that reserved plus settled cost never exceeds the
authorization, with every revealed package, successful or not, billed
at its recorded cost.
