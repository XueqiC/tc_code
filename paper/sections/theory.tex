\section{Theoretical analysis}\label{sec:theory}
%% One page. Brief intro, then two subsections of comparable length:
%% (i) what the transfer optimizes; (ii) what the joint problem and the
%% acquisition value guarantee. Proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}.

We analyze the two components on which the claims of the method rest.
The first subsection characterizes the replacement of
Section~\ref{sec:distill}. It states that the empirical target is a
legal distribution that behaves consistently under relabeling of the
sources, that the estimator keeps the expectation of the target
gradient even though the coefficients are chosen from the same samples
and from feedback, and that the update is affine in the coefficients
with the natural degeneracies. The second subsection states what the
joint problem and the acquisition value do and do not guarantee.
Supporting derivations appear in Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}.

\subsection{What the replacement optimizes}\label{sec:theory-mech}
Fix a unit with teacher text $y^T$, sources $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ drawn
from the snapshot $p_t$, and coefficients $a\in[0,1]^m$. Maximum-
likelihood training on the teacher text alone follows the divergence
between the teacher and student behavior distributions as a whole and
cannot separate the movement the task requires from the replacement
of behaviors the student already had right. The target $\hat q$ of
Section~\ref{sec:distill} makes the replacement explicit per source.

\begin{proposition}[Legal target and consistent estimator]
\label{prop:target}
(i) For every $a\in[0,1]^m$, $\hat q$ is a probability distribution;
merging repeated behaviors only adds their masses. Permuting the
sources together with their coefficients leaves $\hat q$, both
estimators, and the update $\theta^{+}(a)$ unchanged. (ii) The
gradient of the frozen empirical target is $g^{\mathrm{raw}}$, and
$g^{\mathrm{CV}}-g^{\mathrm{raw}}=G^{\mathrm{soft}}-\bar G$ carries no
coefficient. Hence if $\mathbb{E}\,G^{\mathrm{soft}}=\mathbb{E}\,\bar G$
under the source sampling, then for every measurable choice of $a$
from the samples and the feedback,
$\mathbb{E}\,g^{\mathrm{CV}}=\mathbb{E}\,g^{\mathrm{raw}}$, the
expectation being over sources and the selection process jointly.
(iii) Under total-token normalization the identity holds and
$G^{\mathrm{soft}}$ vanishes at $\pi_\theta=p_t$; under per-sequence
length normalization the length-corrected estimator of
Appendix~\ref{app:method} keeps the identity, and no estimator can keep
it and vanish at the snapshot for every sample.
\end{proposition}

Point (ii) is the property that the earlier weighted form
$(1-\alpha)G^{\mathrm{soft}}+\alpha G^T-\tfrac{d}{2}(G_1-G_2)$ lacks
once $\alpha$ depends on the samples; Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}
gives a two-action example in which that form has expectation $-1/4$
while both estimators above have $-3/8$. The identity is
unconditional; given the realized samples and coefficients a single
batch of $g^{\mathrm{CV}}$ need not be the gradient of a non-negative
target, and the endpoint $a=\mathbf{1}$ equals the pure teacher
gradient only in expectation, with a variance we measure rather than
assume away. Point (iii) is why the loss normalization is fixed before
sampling and matched across arms.

\begin{proposition}[Affine update and degeneracies]\label{prop:affine}
With $P$, $\eta$, the samples, and the teacher texts frozen, the
update of Section~\ref{sec:distill} is
$\theta^{+}(a)=\theta_{\mathrm{base}}+Ua$ with
$\theta_{\mathrm{base}}=\theta-\eta P\sum_i w_iG_i^{\mathrm{soft}}$ and
the columns $b_{ij}$. Equal sources give equal columns and a
non-unique but equivalent coefficient split; a source equal to the
teacher gives a zero column; a zero student gradient with a non-zero
teacher gradient leaves $b_{ij}=-(\eta w_i/m)PG_i^T$. A coefficient
vector reached by the solver and a reference vector differ by a
displacement $Ux$ from the common reference.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{What the joint problem and the value guarantee}\label{sec:theory-acq}
The joint problem of Section~\ref{sec:distill} is convex once
$|x_\ell|$ is lifted to auxiliary variables, its Gram $K=U^{\top}HU$ is
positive semidefinite for positive semidefinite $H$, and a solution
is accepted only when primal feasibility, stationarity, and
complementary slackness hold to a recorded tolerance
(Appendix~\ref{app:analysis}). Two consequences shape how the
solution is read. First, the zero condition of coordinate $\ell$ is
$|\hat h^{\top}b_\ell-\lambda\sum_{k\ne\ell}K_{\ell k}x_k|\le\varepsilon_\ell$,
which involves the other coordinates; a source whose own response is
within its uncertainty can still be moved to offset the cost of
another direction, so coordinatewise thresholding would be wrong.
Second, source selection is a strictly larger family than a per-state
scalar dose. Restricting $a_{i1}=a_{i2}$ is a homogeneous equality
constraint on the same problem, and Appendix~\ref{app:analysis} gives
a two-column instance in which the restricted optimum is zero while
the full problem attains a positive value and keeps one behavior
untouched; the comparison class is the scalar restriction on the same
batch and estimator, not every other algorithm.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional local gain]\label{prop:gain}
Let $J$ satisfy a lower smoothness bound with constant $L_H$ in the
$H$-norm on the feasible region around $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$, let
$\lambda\ge L_H$, and let the feedback errors be controlled
simultaneously, $|b_\ell^{\top}(\hat h-h)|\le\varepsilon_\ell$ for all
$\ell$. Then for every feasible $x$,
$J(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}+Ux)-J(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})\ge F(x)$.
If the realized displacement is $Ux+e$ with $\|e\|_H\le\rho$ and
$\|h\|_{H,*}\le M$, the bound loses
$(M+L_H\|Ux\|_H)\rho+L_H\rho^2/2$; an approximate solution within
$\delta$ of the optimum loses $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

The bound is the origin of the surrogate, and its assumptions are the
reason we call $F$ a local proxy rather than a guarantee. The
uncertainty terms are per-direction standard errors of a finite
rollout batch; they are not simultaneous confidence bounds after
selection, the smoothness constant is unknown, and between decision
windows the estimate is stale, which the implementation records as
feedback age and does not correct. Realized paired gains on a feedback
batch that did not choose $x$ are therefore the validation we report,
and an independent confirmation split remains necessary. For
acquisition, the value $A(Q)$ of Section~\ref{sec:acquire} is a
difference of optima of nested convex problems, so it is non-negative
and equals zero exactly when the new directions receive zero use at
the optimum; it counts the base teaching the new package makes
possible, and it is a local one-step quantity that neither includes
future information gain nor certifies stopping. The predictor that
estimates $A(Q)$ before purchase reads only pre-purchase information,
and the budget itself is enforced by the ledger invariant that
reserved plus settled cost never exceeds the authorization, with every
revealed package, successful or not, billed at its recorded cost.
