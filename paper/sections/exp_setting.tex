\paragraph{Benchmarks and evaluation.}
We evaluate on four agent benchmarks, each under its official harness
and metric. \textbf{AppWorld} \citep{trivedi2024appworld} tasks an
agent with day-to-day objectives across nine simulated applications;
the main-text column reports the unit-test pass ratio under the
official evaluator, with task and scenario goal completion (TGC and
SGC) alongside at the test\_normal freeze.
\todo{finalize the frozen AppWorld protocol numbers.}
\textbf{BFCL~v4} evaluates function calling across single-turn,
parallel, and multi-turn categories, reported as the official overall
accuracy. \textbf{$\tau^2$-bench} \citep{yao2024taubench} places the
agent in customer-service conversations with database-backed tools and
a simulated user, reported as pass\textsuperscript{1}, with the user
simulator served by the same metered teacher so its tokens are charged
against the budget. \textbf{ALFWorld} \citep{shridhar2021alfworld}
tests embodied household instruction following, reported as success
rate on the unseen split. All numbers are mean$\pm$std over three
seeds, with the change relative to the untrained base student made
explicit. Budget efficiency is measured by the wasted fraction of
purchased tokens and by the budget-to-target level $B^{*}(\tau)$, the
smallest budget at which a method reaches target performance $\tau$;
off-task behavior is reported as residue on scoped reference tasks and
read as evidence of where the budget went rather than as an objective.

\paragraph{Few-shot instantiation.}
Each benchmark instantiates the setting of
Definition~\ref{def:setting} independently, following the standard
support and query division of few-shot learning evaluation
\citep{vinyals2016matching, snell2017prototypical}. The support set
$\mathcal{S}$ is sampled once per benchmark with a fixed seed,
stratified over the benchmark's task categories, and every method,
including the base student rows, is evaluated on the query side only.
The pool from which $\mathcal{S}$ is drawn is the benchmark's official
training split when one exists, and the full task set otherwise, in
which case the support queries are removed from evaluation. The
support size $N$ is five percent of this pool clipped to the range
$[50, 250]$, matching the demonstration volumes used by agent
distillation work \citep{kang2025distilling, liu2025structured};
eighty percent of $\mathcal{S}$ forms $\mathcal{S}_d$, the only data
available for demand estimation, acquisition, and training, and the
rest forms $\mathcal{S}_c$, reserved for boundary calibration.
Per-benchmark sizes are listed in Table~\ref{tab:support-sizes} in
the appendix. The budget $B$ counts every teacher output token,
including completions that fail verification and are discarded;
retries are charged, and on $\tau^2$-bench the user-simulator tokens
are metered separately from the supervision budget. Teacher-using
methods are matched at the same $B$, while teacher-free baselines
such as STaR follow their native zero-teacher protocol.

\paragraph{Baselines and implementation.}
Following the standard taxonomy of LLM distillation
\citep{xu2024kdsurvey}, the main comparison covers each black-box
branch with current representatives, all consuming the same budget
from the same teacher; feature-based white-box methods require teacher
logits and fall outside the setting. From the labeling branch we take
\emph{Vanilla SFT} \citep{mukherjee2023orca}, \emph{Structured Agent
Distillation} \citep{liu2025structured}, and the agent-domain
representative of \citet{kang2025distilling}; from the feedback branch
\emph{dDPO} \citep{tunstall2023zephyr} and \emph{PBSD}
\citep{pbsd2026}; from the self-knowledge branch \emph{STaR}
\citep{zelikman2022star}; and from the on-policy branch
\emph{Black-Box On-Policy Distillation} \citep{ye2025bbopd}.
Component studies additionally use selection and data-generation
baselines spanning classical and current families, listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}. All main-text experiments use one fixed
pair, a metered black-box teacher (DeepSeek-V4-Pro via API, exposing
neither logits nor hidden states nor gradients) and a Qwen3.5-4B
student; other student scales appear in Appendix~\ref{app:details}.
Students train with LoRA adapters (rank 16) under compute-matched
schedules; every threshold in the method is a statistical convention
or is estimated from the support set, with full constants in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}. Experiments run on a local server with
five mixed NVIDIA GPUs (48--96GB) for simulator-bound evaluation and
on NVIDIA B200 nodes for training sweeps; a full run of one method on
one benchmark takes two to five GPU hours at 4B.
