\paragraph{Benchmarks, baselines, and evaluation.}
The main table uses three agent benchmarks under their official
harnesses, with every method deployed through the same tools, prompts,
action retries, and decoding, so that only the student backbone
changes: \textbf{ALFWorld} \citep{shridhar2021alfworld} (success rate
on the 140-task \texttt{valid\_seen} split),
\textbf{HotpotQA-ReAct} \citep{yang2018hotpotqa, yao2023react}
(exact match on 500 development questions under a Wikipedia search
tool, with F1 reported as a diagnostic), and \textbf{BFCL~v4}
(official overall accuracy over the full 5{,}217-entry suite). The
first two are the benchmarks used by structured agent distillation
\citep{liu2025structured}, and the third adds a function-calling
suite. WebShop \citep{yao2022webshop} is excluded because the teacher
itself succeeds on one instruction in five under its official
verifier, so a purchased demonstration there certifies little. AppWorld
\citep{trivedi2024appworld} and $\tau^2$-bench \citep{yao2024taubench}
are not in the main table; the former floors every small student under
its simplified harness and costs about $10^5$ teacher tokens per
demonstration under its official scaffold, and the latter meters a
simulated user at a similar cost, so neither fits a supervision budget
of the size studied here. Protocols and task counts are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}. The baselines carry the names of the
methods they adapt, and Appendix~\ref{app:baselines} gives their
losses, data, and what each adaptation keeps of the original:
\emph{SmartAD} (demonstration selection by student difficulty),
\emph{SAD} (Structured Agent Distillation, \citealp{liu2025structured}),
\emph{Agent Distillation} \citep{kang2025distilling} in its
retrieval-free form, and \emph{GAD} (generative adversarial
distillation, \citealp{ye2025bbopd}), which reuses purchased teacher
text through a discriminator reward. All four share one absolute
teacher budget cap of $B=30{,}000$ output tokens, identical across
benchmarks, and every teacher output token they consume, including
discarded failures, ranking calls, and on-policy corrections, is
charged against it. Each trained row is
mean$\pm$std over three training runs (data order, adapter
initialization, and the sampling of the student itself) under the
official decoding of each benchmark. The \emph{initial student} row is
a single reference evaluation of the released checkpoint on the same
machine and configuration, with its evaluation-repeat spread stated
separately in Appendix~\ref{app:details}, and improvements are
reported in percentage points over it on the full fixed column set.

\paragraph{Few-shot setup.}
Each benchmark instantiates Definition~\ref{def:setting}
independently, following the support and query division of classical
few-shot evaluation \citep{vinyals2016matching, snell2017prototypical}.
The support set $\mathcal{S}$ is drawn once with a fixed seed,
stratified over task categories, from the official training split when
one exists and from the full task set otherwise, and all methods,
including the initial student, are evaluated on the query side only.
The support size is five percent of the pool clipped to $[50,250]$,
which matches the demonstration volumes of agent distillation work
\citep{kang2025distilling, liu2025structured}; eighty percent of
$\mathcal{S}$ forms the working split $\mathcal{S}_d$ and the
remainder the calibration split $\mathcal{S}_c$, with per-benchmark
sizes in Table~\ref{tab:support-sizes}. The budget $B$ counts every
teacher output token including discarded failures, teacher-using
methods are matched at the same $B$, and teacher-free baselines follow
their native zero-teacher protocol.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
All main-text experiments use one fixed pair, a metered black-box
teacher (GPT-5.6 Luna through the OpenAI API, exposing neither logits nor
hidden states nor gradients, billed per output token including any
reasoning tokens) and a Gemma-4-12B instruction-tuned student
\citep{gemma4}. The student is a dense 12B model with native
structured tool calling, so BFCL prompts and targets are rendered in
its own tool-call format and ALFWorld and HotpotQA-ReAct use its chat template
with plain-text actions; training prompts are byte-identical to the
strings the evaluators send. Students train LoRA adapters (rank 16)
under compute-matched schedules, and every threshold in the method is
either a statistical convention or is estimated from the support set,
with all constants listed in Appendix~\ref{app:details}. Experiments
run on a shared lab server with mixed NVIDIA GPUs (48--96GB); one
method on one benchmark takes \todo{X} GPU hours at 12B.
