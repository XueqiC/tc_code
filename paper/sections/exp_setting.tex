\paragraph{Benchmarks, baselines, and evaluation.}
The main table uses three agent benchmarks under their official
harnesses, with every method deployed through the same tools, prompts,
action retries, and decoding, so that only the 4B backbone changes:
\textbf{AppWorld} \citep{trivedi2024appworld} (task goal completion,
TGC, on a fixed 40-task development subset with a 24-step cap;
the unit-test pass ratio is reported as a diagnostic), \textbf{BFCL~v4}
(official overall accuracy over the full 5{,}217-entry suite), and
\textbf{ALFWorld} \citep{shridhar2021alfworld} (success rate on the
140-task \texttt{valid\_seen} split). $\tau^2$-bench
\citep{yao2024taubench} is excluded from the main table because its
simulated user consumes an order of magnitude more teacher tokens per
episode than any supervision budget studied here; protocols and task
counts are given in Appendix~\ref{app:details}. The baselines carry the names of the methods they adapt, and
Appendix~\ref{app:baselines} gives their losses, data, and what each
adaptation keeps of the original:
\emph{SFT} (trajectory imitation, \citealp{mukherjee2023orca}),
\emph{SAD} (Structured Agent Distillation, \citealp{liu2025structured}),
\emph{BBOPD} (the supervised core of black-box on-policy distillation,
\citealp{ye2025bbopd}, without its discriminator), \emph{dDPO}
\citep{tunstall2023zephyr}, \emph{PBSD} \citep{pbsd2026} in its agent
adaptation, and \emph{STaR}
without rationalization \citep{zelikman2022star}. Teacher-using methods share the same teacher
budget cap $B$ on a benchmark, and every teacher output token they
consume, including discarded failures, ranking calls, and on-policy
corrections, is charged against it; STaR uses no teacher call and is
reported as the zero-teacher reference. Each trained row is
mean$\pm$std over three training runs (data order, adapter
initialization, and the sampling of the student itself) under the
official decoding of each benchmark. The \emph{initial pretrained
student} row is a single reference evaluation of the released
checkpoint on the same machine and configuration, with its
evaluation-repeat spread stated separately in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}, and improvements are reported in
percentage points over it on the full fixed column set.

\paragraph{Few-shot setup.}
Each benchmark instantiates Definition~\ref{def:setting}
independently, following the support and query division of classical
few-shot evaluation \citep{vinyals2016matching, snell2017prototypical}.
The support set $\mathcal{S}$ is drawn once with a fixed seed,
stratified over task categories, from the official training split when
one exists and from the full task set otherwise, and all methods,
including the base student, are evaluated on the query side only. The
support size is five percent of the pool clipped to $[50,250]$, which
matches the demonstration volumes of agent distillation work
\citep{kang2025distilling, liu2025structured}; eighty percent of
$\mathcal{S}$ forms the working split $\mathcal{S}_d$ and the
remainder the calibration split $\mathcal{S}_c$, with per-benchmark
sizes in Table~\ref{tab:support-sizes}. The budget $B$ counts every
teacher output token including discarded failures, teacher-using
methods are matched at the same $B$, and teacher-free baselines follow
their native zero-teacher protocol.

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
All main-text experiments use one fixed pair, a metered black-box
teacher (DeepSeek-V4-Pro through its API, exposing neither logits nor
hidden states nor gradients) and a Qwen3.5-4B student; other student
scales appear in Appendix~\ref{app:details}. Students train LoRA
adapters (rank 16) under compute-matched schedules, and every
threshold in the method is either a statistical convention or is
estimated from the support set, with all constants listed in
Appendix~\ref{app:details}. Experiments run on a local server with
five mixed NVIDIA GPUs (48--96GB) for simulator-bound evaluation and
on NVIDIA B200 nodes for training sweeps; one method on one benchmark
takes two to five GPU hours at 4B.
