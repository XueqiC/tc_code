We organize the evaluation around five research questions.
\textbf{RQ1} asks whether the full method improves few-shot agent
specialization under a common teacher budget cap. \textbf{RQ2} asks
whether choosing the replacement per source by the learning return
beats a per-state teacher dose and the earlier block procedure on
identical purchased data. \textbf{RQ3} asks how
much acquisition by the joint-update surrogate contributes when the
training procedure is held fixed. \textbf{RQ4} asks how much the
distillation procedure contributes on identical purchased data.
\textbf{RQ5} asks which components are necessary and how efficiently
the teacher budget is converted into task capability. The following
subsections answer these questions in turn.

\subsection{RQ2, does per-source replacement beat a per-state dose}
\label{sec:rq2}
The mechanism claim of Section~\ref{sec:distill} is that choosing,
per sampled behavior, how much to hand to the teacher by the learning
return improves on a per-state teacher dose and on the earlier
procedure that fixed the dose by a gate and chose the split
separately. We test it with arms that share the initialization, the
purchased pool, the exposure schedule, the source samples, and the
feedback protocol, and differ only in the control
(Appendix~\ref{app:details}): the full source replacement with the
exact local solver (D3); the same estimator and solver under the
restriction $a_{i1}=a_{i2}$, one dose per state solved jointly (D2);
and the earlier block procedure with a state-only gate and a separate
split solver (D1). \todo{fill from the P1 fixed-pool runs.} The
comparison is falsifiable in both directions. If D3 does not separate
from D2, the evidence supports reweighting but not source selection;
if the realized gain of D3 is indistinguishable from a shuffled source
pairing, the solved shares carry no information beyond the dose.

\subsection{RQ4, value of the training channel on identical data}
\label{sec:rq4}
Table~\ref{tab:distill} feeds the identical purchased evidence to
every distillation baseline of Table~\ref{tab:main} and to ours, so
whatever difference remains can only come from the training
procedure. The fixed-purchased-data control of the protocol serves the
same purpose in the other direction; it trains our procedure on the
purchases of the random control arm. \todo{fill from the distillation
matrix on the frozen purchased set.} The comparison predicted by the
mechanism is that procedures that replace the behaviors of the student
wholesale lose part of what the initial student could do before they
finish absorbing the teacher, while the transfer with fixed teacher
mass and a return-chosen replacement keeps it.

\subsection{RQ5, components and budget conversion}
\label{sec:rq5}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} attributes the gain of the full method on one
purchased pool. The first three rows change the training procedure
only, and the variants below D3 remove one element each: the cross
terms of the interaction matrix, the control-variate estimator, the
correspondence between behaviors and directions, and the freedom of
the mean share. \todo{fill once the P1 runs land.} The reading rules
are fixed in advance. A gain over D1 but not over D2 supports
reweighting rather than selection, a gain only over the high-dose
variants supports stability, and a gain that the shuffled pairing
reproduces is not evidence for the replacement decision.
