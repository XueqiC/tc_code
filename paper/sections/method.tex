\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig1_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{The learning pipeline in three stages. \textbf{Stage I}: one
backward pass turns each support example into a gradient feature; sparse
dictionary learning yields capability atoms (Definition~\ref{def:atom}),
the support set becomes a task demand (Definition~\ref{def:demand}), and
the budget is allocated per skill. \textbf{Stage II}: an acquisition
loop queries the pay-per-token teacher where unallocated demand is
largest, filters returns through an execution check and a
dictionary-reconstruction membership test, reads the current student's
skill deficits from its training dynamics, and closes them by targeted
selection, falling back to targeted generation when the candidate set
cannot supply a deficient skill. \textbf{Stage III}: on-policy
distillation. The training signal is stratified by the student's own
policy state: self-solves are kept, own failures are pushed down
against their corrections, and demonstrations are cloned only where no
attempt exists; one verifier-anchored objective is optimized and
finished with weight averaging of near-best probe checkpoints. The trained student can then be equipped
with a conformal deployment gate carrying finite-sample two-sided
certificates.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the learning pipeline. No training
corpus is given in our setting, so the method cannot rank an existing
pool; it must decide what to ask a metered teacher, one paid query at a
time. We therefore cast few-shot distillation as active learning
against a pay-per-token teacher \citep{settles2009active}, built from
three stages that share one learned object, the skill vocabulary, and
one principle: the student attempts first, a verifier grades for free,
and teacher supervision is bought only where the student's own policy
fails. A from-scratch demonstration is the degenerate case of this
principle, the repair of an empty attempt, so as the student improves,
purchases shift toward corrections of its own solutions, and the whole
pipeline becomes on-policy distillation under a metered black-box
teacher \citep{ross2011dagger}. Stage I extracts the skill vocabulary
from the student's own gradients and allocates the budget per skill.
Stage II greedily maximizes a budgeted submodular skill-coverage
objective over a view-invariant sparse code. Stage III stratifies the
training signal by the student's policy state and optimizes one
verifier-anchored objective. Every threshold is either a statistical
convention or estimated from the support set itself; no component is
tuned per benchmark.

\subsection{Problem setting: few-shot agent distillation}\label{sec:setting}
A task is a distribution $P_\mathcal{T}$ over queries, revealed only
through a \emph{support set} $S = \{q_1, \dots, q_k\} \sim P_\mathcal{T}$,
which is the sole task-specific information any method receives. Every method
additionally gets the same three resources: a teacher agent $\pi_T$,
accessed strictly as a black-box API that returns sampled trajectories and
charges per emitted token; an open-weight student $\theta_0$; and a
token budget. This is the regime of closed commercial models, and it
rules out distribution-matching distillation \citep{gu2024minillm,
agarwal2024gkd}. The budget
$b$ caps all teacher tokens purchased over the entire run. There is no
pre-existing corpus: every demonstration must be generated by querying
$\pi_T$, every returned token is charged against $b$, and the support set
is never expanded.

From $(S, \pi_T, \theta_0, b)$ a method must produce a boundary predictor
$\hat{B}$ with distribution-free coverage
$\Pr_{q\sim P_\mathcal{T}}[\hat{B}(q)=\text{in}] \ge 1-\alpha$, and a
student evaluated two-sidedly: coverage risk, meaning in-domain
failures, and leakage, meaning out-of-domain capability, under matched
$(S, \pi_T, b)$,
together with the budget-to-target level $B^{*}(\tau)$ and the wasted
fraction of purchased tokens. Two assumptions: \textbf{A1} the student's
gradients are accessible, and \textbf{A2} some check such as an
executor, a unit test, or a judge decides success. Code-acting agents are the instantiation we
evaluate, not a requirement.

\subsection{Gradient features: reading what an example teaches}
\label{sec:fingerprints}
Every example the method touches, each support query, and later each
purchased demonstration, is represented by its \emph{gradient feature}
\citep{xia2024less}: the Adam-preconditioned gradient that the example
induces on the student, compressed by a fixed random projection. The
representational point is that the gradient describes an example by what
the student would have to \emph{learn} from it, not by which words appear
in it. Identical query texts solved in two different styles are
inseparable to any function of the text, yet their gradients separate
perfectly: hard-pair AUROC is $1.000$ against $0.687$ for text
embeddings in Section~\ref{sec:boundary}. Following
\citet{xia2024less}, a brief low-rank warmup on the first purchased
demonstrations accumulates the optimizer's second-moment statistics and absorbs
surface formatting; each example then contributes one backward pass, and
the adapter gradient is rescaled coordinate-wise by the inverse root of
the second moments, so the feature measures the update step the optimizer
would actually take. The random projection preserves the inner products
that all downstream computation consumes, up to $(1\pm\varepsilon)$
\citep{johnson1984extensions}. One distinction matters under the metered
protocol: for a purchased demonstration the feature is computed on the
teacher's response, whereas for a candidate prompt whose answer has not
been bought yet, only the feature of the \emph{prompt text} is
available. The acquisition step below is careful to plan with the
latter and account with the former.

\subsection{Capability atoms and task demand}\label{sec:atoms}
Gradient features of related examples are not arbitrary vectors: they
reuse a small set of recurring directions, the way recipes reuse a small
set of basic operations. Sparse dictionary learning
\citep{olshausen1996emergence} recovers exactly this structure, and
one design decision makes it serve planning under the metered protocol.
Which skills an example exercises should not depend on which view of
the example is visible: the prompt and the response are two projections
of the same latent skill profile. We therefore learn a \emph{two-view
coupled dictionary} \citep{yang2010coupled}: writing the prompt-view
and response-view feature matrices as $X_p, X_r$, we solve
\[
\min_{D_p, D_r, C}\ \lVert X_p - CD_p\rVert_2^2
+ \lVert X_r - CD_r\rVert_2^2 + \lambda\lVert C\rVert_1,
\]
so a \emph{single} sparse code per example must reconstruct both of its
views, alternating a lasso step with a dictionary update.

\begin{definition}[Capability atom]\label{def:atom}
A \emph{capability atom} is one row $[d_p; d_r]$ of the learned coupled
dictionary: a paired direction in prompt- and response-view
gradient-feature space. The sparse code $c(x)$ of an example is its
coefficient vector over the atoms; $|c(x)|_a$ measures how strongly
example $x$ exercises atom $a$, and is computable from the prompt view
alone, a lasso solve against the $D_p$ block, for candidates
whose response has not been purchased.
\end{definition}

The shared code is what removes the need for any learned bridge between
views: support demonstrations are free and purchased examples
accumulate, so response information shapes the dictionary at fit time,
and a
candidate's prompt-only code inherits it. A post-hoc regression from
prompt to response features is the ablated alternative; its per-atom
predictions are unreliable precisely because response structure never
enters its fitting objective.

The $\ell_1$ penalty is what makes atoms identifiable, and it is the
design choice that separates this component from PCA or SVD: a subspace is
invariant to rotations of its basis, so no individual principal direction
can be identified with a skill, whereas rotating a dictionary destroys
the sparsity of its codes. The only bases that survive the penalty are
those aligned with recurring, self-contained gradient patterns, and
those patterns behave like skills. Empirically, atom support separates
fine-grained skills within one domain at AUROC $0.906$ where the dense
subspace reaches $0.615$, and the behavioral change caused by
distillation concentrates $85.5\%$ of its mass on $5$ of $128$ atoms
(Section~\ref{sec:atoms-results}). Because no corpus is given, the
dictionary cannot be pretrained: it is fit first on the support features
alone and refit as purchased demonstrations accumulate, so the atom count
grows with the evidence, and a demonstration the current dictionary
reconstructs poorly signals, through its residual, that a new skill has
appeared.

\begin{definition}[Task demand]\label{def:demand}
Given a dictionary $D$ and the support set $S$, the \emph{task demand} is
$w = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{q\in S} |c(q)|$, normalized to sum to one and
restricted to the atoms carrying $90\%$ of its mass. Scaled by the
budget, $w \cdot b$ assigns each atom a token quota: the number of
training tokens the task asks the method to spend on that skill.
\end{definition}

The support set is a small sample, so $w$ inherits its blind spots. The
teacher's own solutions correct part of them: atoms that appear
persistently in the codes of demonstrations for support queries, yet
carry little weight in $w$, are skills the task requires
\emph{implicitly}: the query said ``aggregate by month'' while every
competent solution also plotted. Their demand is filled in from the
demonstration side
before further budget is spent. This completion touches only how the
budget is allocated; the boundary and all certificates remain calibrated
on the original $k$ queries alone.

\subsection{Stage II: budgeted data acquisition}
\label{sec:acquire}
Acquisition runs in rounds of a fixed, task-agnostic batch size of
$B{=}8$ and alternates with training; the budget is split evenly across
$R$ rounds, with $R{=}2$ unless stated. \textbf{Update
residual demand.} At each round the dictionary is refit on the support
features together with everything acquired so far, the vocabulary
sharpens as evidence accumulates (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}
quantifies this: a $k{=}50$ support set alone recovers $67\%$ of the
full-corpus vocabulary; after one acquisition round the refit dictionary
recovers $84\%$), and each acquired example's contribution,
$|c(x)|_a \cdot t(x) / \kappa$ with $\kappa$ the mean code mass of
verified in-task examples, is subtracted from the per-atom allocation.
Code magnitudes are deliberately \emph{not} normalized per example: the
lasso assigns small codes to unrelated examples, and that magnitude is
itself the relevance signal. \textbf{Code the candidates.} A candidate
prompt is coded against the $D_p$ block of the coupled dictionary
(Definition~\ref{def:atom}); the shared-code constraint makes this
prompt-only code the prediction $\hat{s}(x)$ of which atoms the unseen
answer would exercise, no separate regression. \textbf{Select and
query.} Acquisition is the budgeted maximization of a skill-coverage
objective: over a purchase set $A$,
\[
F(A) \;=\; \sum_a \min\!\Big(w_a b,\ \textstyle\sum_{x\in A}
s_a(x)\Big),
\]
a monotone submodular function, since a modular supply passes through
a concave truncation, so greedily buying
\[
\argmax_x\; u(x) \;=\; \frac{\sum_a \min\!\big(r_a,\
\hat{s}_a(x)\big)}{\hat{t}}
\]
with $r_a$ the remaining allocation and $\hat{t}$ the running mean
response length, is exactly cost-normalized greedy maximization of
$F$ and inherits its near-optimality guarantees
\citep{khuller1999budgeted, krause2005near}. Within a batch each
selection immediately decrements a working copy of $r$, so
near-duplicates do not double-count. \textbf{Filter.} Every
return must pass the execution check of A2 and a membership test: the
prompt feature's sparse-reconstruction residual under a dictionary fit
to the support prompts must not exceed that of the least typical
calibration query, the task boundary \emph{is} what the skill
vocabulary can express, and an optional slack of $s$ standard deviations
traces a purity--performance frontier. Filtered returns are still paid
for, because the budget is real, but they never train the student; the
wasted fraction is reported. \textbf{Close the deficits, attempt-first.} After each training
interlude with the Stage III recipe, the student's remaining deficits
are read
from its per-atom training dynamics, atoms whose loss has not come
down, without any rollouts; the next round's ranking direction
becomes the demand restricted to those atoms. Before any money is
spent, the intermediate student \emph{attempts} the shortlisted
candidates, three samples each. A candidate it solves consistently,
meaning all samples execute and agree, is self-solved: its own solution
joins the corpus at zero teacher cost, and the freed budget moves down
the ranking. Purchases are reserved for genuine failures and take two
forms: a fresh demonstration, or, for questions whose reference
answer is already owned, a \emph{correction}: the teacher minimally
repairs the student's own wrong solution, yielding an on-policy
demonstration whose edit is localized to the mistake. Either purchase
pairs the student's failed attempt with the correct solution, supplying
Stage III's preference pass for free. A rollout-free deficit variant
matches accuracy at lower cost when attempts are disabled and is
reported as an ablation. Where the remaining candidates cannot supply a
deficient skill, the teacher is asked to \emph{generate}: the support
queries loading highest on that atom serve as exemplars, the teacher
writes new problems and execution-verified solutions, all tokens
charged, and generated and selected candidates compete in the same
ranking. Where \textsc{DAgger} \citep{ross2011dagger}
forwards failure states and LLM2LLM \citep{lee2024llm2llm} augments near
failure instances, deficits here live in skill space: correction spreads
across a skill's support, buying measurably more distinct demonstrations
per token at matched budget and halving across-seed variance
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). The contrast with influence-based
selection against a single mean target \citep{xia2024less} is
structural: collapsing a multi-skill task onto one direction leaves
surplus tokens nowhere to go but out of scope, whereas per-skill
allocations give every token a named destination.

\subsection{Stage III: on-policy distillation}
\label{sec:distill}
Stage III turns the acquired evidence into weights, and its
organization follows one rule: \emph{the training signal an example
contributes is determined by the student's own policy state on it}.
Each acquired question falls into one of three strata. Questions the
intermediate student solved consistently contribute its \emph{own}
solution as a plain likelihood term, preserving verified behavior at
zero teacher cost, a budgeted analogue of self-improvement
bootstrapping \citep{zelikman2022star}. Questions the student attempted
and failed contribute the purchased solution as the likelihood term
\emph{plus} a preference term pushing down the student's own failed
attempt. Questions the student could not attempt at all contribute pure
behavior cloning, the degenerate stratum, mirroring Stage II's
``a demonstration is the repair of an empty attempt.'' All three strata
optimize a single objective,
\[
\mathcal{L}(x) \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{NLL}\big(y^{+}\,|\,x\big)}_{
\text{teacher or self solution}} \;+\; \beta\,\mathbb{1}\big[y^{-}
\text{ exists}\big]\cdot
\ell_{\mathrm{OR}}\big(y^{+}\!\succ y^{-}\,|\,x\big),
\]
in one compute-matched pass: smaller corpora receive proportionally
more epochs, and the loss is deliberately \emph{not} reweighted by
demand, controlled comparisons show that on execution-verified,
in-task corpora, neither demand weighting nor demand-ordered curricula
improve over uniformly shuffled training, and a misordered curriculum
reliably hurts (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}); both are reported as
ablations. As the student improves, the first stratum grows and the
third shrinks, so training becomes progressively more on-policy for
free.

\textbf{Skill-resolved observability.} Every acquired demonstration
carries its dominant-atom label, so per-example training losses
aggregate, at no extra cost, into per-atom absorption curves: the
loss trajectory of each individual skill. Most atoms' losses collapse
within the first epochs while a small, seed-dependent set lags; the
curves are what Stage II's deficit reads consume, and they make the
distillation process inspectable at the skill level
; the appendix plots them.

\textbf{The preference term.} Supervised fine-tuning repeats what the
teacher did; it never tells the student what \emph{it itself} does
wrong. The failed attempts carry exactly that information, and the
teacher exposes no log-probabilities, so $\ell_{\mathrm{OR}}$ is a
reference-model-free odds-ratio objective \citep{hong2024orpo} rather
than \textsc{DPO} \citep{rafailov2023dpo}. Three properties make the
term robust without tuned constants. First, an attempt and its
correction share a long common prefix, and sequence-level preference
pressure on shared tokens is precisely what drives \emph{likelihood
displacement}, near-identical pairs drag the preferred response down
with the rejected one \citep{razin2025unintentional}; the term is
therefore computed only on tokens from the first point of divergence,
removing the displacement pressure at its source. Second, embedding the
term in the same pass as the likelihood objective anchors it: an
isolated preference phase run on the same pairs collapses answer
accuracy within a handful of steps, while co-training remains stable
; each step's supervised gradient is the anchor that a bolt-on phase
lacks. Third, a safety net with no new constants: every few steps,
execution \emph{correctness} is re-measured on the calibration queries
whose gold answers come free from executing the teacher's own
demonstrations, and the term is halted on the first drop exceeding one
probe question; runnability alone is blind to accuracy collapse under
preference pressure. In controlled comparisons the same rollout data
helps as preference negatives yet adds nothing as extra positives: the
marginal value of self-generated data lies in its failures.

\textbf{Checkpoint averaging and budget-agnostic rollback.} A probe
every $8$ steps measures the fraction of held-out calibration queries
yielding runnable solutions and records near-best checkpoints. At the
end of training their weights are averaged with the final state
\citep{izmailov2018swa} and the average is adopted if the probe does
not object, across-seed variance is the dominant noise source at
this scale, and weight averaging is a free variance reducer. The best
recorded checkpoint replaces the result \emph{only if it wins by more
than one probe question}: a genuine small-budget collapse exceeds this
margin by far and is rescued, while at large budgets the difference is
probe noise. One rule for every budget; no regime split. Throughout,
gradient geometry is never asked to judge success, because three
pre-registered experiments in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} show it
cannot; the atoms diagnose \emph{what to acquire
and observe}, while behavior remains the sole judge of arrival.

\subsection{Certified deployment (a configuration, not a stage)}
\label{sec:behavior}
The learning pipeline ends with Stage III; the trained student can
additionally ship as a deliverable: student, boundary predictor, and
two-sided certificates. Because a deployment-time request carries no
response, the boundary predictor lives in prompt-feature space and
reuses Stage II's membership machinery: the sparse-reconstruction
residual under the support-prompt dictionary is the nonconformity
score, and a conformal threshold from a disjoint calibration split
gives distribution-free coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$
\citep{angelopoulos2023conformal}; the earlier subspace-residual score
is kept as an ablation. The student is
then evaluated behaviorally on both sides of the boundary and the
capability match is stated as finite-sample Clopper--Pearson bounds: a
certified lower bound on in-task coverage and a certified upper bound on
off-task leakage, a bounded claim, not a score. At deployment the same
machinery gates live requests: in-specification queries route to the
student, the rest are refused or escalated to the teacher, so the
certificates describe exactly the traffic the student will see.
