\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}\label{sec:setting}

We study few-shot task specialization of a language-model agent under
a limited teacher budget. Let $\mathcal{D}$ denote the target query
distribution. A support set $\mathcal{S}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ with
$x_i\sim\mathcal{D}$ is the deployer's finite observation of the
task; $\mathcal{D}$ itself is accessed only through the held-out
benchmark evaluation. An agent executes a trajectory
$\tau=(a_1,o_1,\ldots,a_H,o_H)$, where $a_t$ is the model action at
step $t$ and $o_t$ the environment observation, and a task verifier
$V(x,\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ evaluates whether the trajectory solves $x$.

Starting from a generic student policy $\pi_{\theta_0}$ and given
query access to a stronger teacher policy $\pi_\phi$ whose calls are
billed per emitted token, the problem is
\[
\max_{\theta}\;
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}\,
\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[V(x,\tau)\bigr]
\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
\textstyle\sum_{d\in\mathcal{A}}c(d)\le B,
\]
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of teacher completions purchased during
training and $c(d)$ their token cost. Every teacher call is charged,
including completions that fail verification.

The conventional approach uses a purchased demonstration
$d\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x)$ directly as an imitation target. Such an
update moves the student toward the teacher distribution, since
\[
\mathbb{E}_{d\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x)}
\bigl[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(d\mid x)\bigr]
=-\nabla_\theta\,
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\bigl(\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\bigr),
\]
and nothing in this objective separates task-relevant capability
transfer from changes induced merely by the difference between
teacher and student behavior distributions. Our method instead uses
the teacher only to facilitate exploration. A purchased demonstration
is converted into a context $h(d)$ under which the student generates
new trajectories, and teacher tokens are never used as optimization
targets. The problem is then to decide where to spend teacher tokens,
when teacher context is needed for exploration, and how guided
student trajectories improve the unguided deployment policy. We call
the resulting framework budgeted verifier-guided self-distillation
with teacher context scaffolding. Before training we partition
$\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_d\cup\mathcal{S}_c$ with
$|\mathcal{S}_d|=40$ and $|\mathcal{S}_c|=10$; only $\mathcal{S}_d$
enters task characterization, acquisition, and optimization, while
$\mathcal{S}_c$ is reserved for post-training boundary calibration.

\subsection{Task-aware teacher acquisition}\label{sec:atoms}
\label{sec:acquire}

Because demonstrations serve as exploration scaffolds rather than
training targets, their value lies in unlocking successful student
behavior in regions the current student has not mastered. Crucially,
a candidate for purchase is a query, not a demonstration, since the
teacher's completion is unknown until it is paid for.

\paragraph{Representing task demand.}
We characterize the task in the student's parameter space. For each
support query $x\in\mathcal{S}_d$ we extract a gradient feature
$g(x)\in\mathbb{R}^p$, the Adam-preconditioned gradient of the causal
language-modeling loss of the query text itself under the student's
restricted parameter subset, compressed by a fixed random projection
\citep{xia2024less, johnson1984extensions}; this probe objective
requires no labels, and Appendix~\ref{app:method} gives details. We
fit a sparse dictionary $D=[d_1,\ldots,d_M]$
\citep{olshausen1996emergence} and code each query as
\[
z(x)=\arg\min_z\ \|g(x)-Dz\|_2^2+\lambda_z\|z\|_1.
\]
Aggregating activations over $\mathcal{S}_d$ yields a demand vector
$r=(r_1,\ldots,r_M)$ whose active directions span an estimated
task-demand subspace $\mathcal{C}_\mathcal{T}$. The same
representation provides a diagnostic for supervision-induced drift.
For an expected update $u$, we decompose
$u=\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_\mathcal{T}}u+(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_\mathcal{T}})u$
and define
\[
R_{\mathrm{ext}}(u)
=\frac{\bigl\|(I-\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_\mathcal{T}})u\bigr\|_2}
       {\bigl\|\Pi_{\mathcal{C}_\mathcal{T}}u\bigr\|_2+\epsilon},
\]
an analysis tool that does not presume dictionary atoms intrinsically
separate capability from mismatch; Section~\ref{sec:rq2} tests whether
it predicts degradation.

\paragraph{Valuing a candidate query.}
At round $r$, let $s_{r,j}$ be the verified student capability
accumulated along direction $d_j$ and
$\delta_{r,j}=(r_j-s_{r,j})_+$ the remaining deficit. For a candidate
query $x$ we form pre-purchase estimates in expectation over the
unknown completion,
\[
\hat s_{r,j}(x)\approx
\mathbb{E}_{d\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[s_j(d)\bigr],
\qquad
\hat\rho_r(x)\approx
\mathbb{E}_{d\sim\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid x)}
\Bigl[\Pr_{\tau\sim\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x,h(d))}
\bigl[V(x,\tau)=1\bigr]\Bigr],
\]
with a common prior before any guided observation and online updates
from observed purchases and rollouts thereafter. The expected
unlocked coverage is
$C_r(x)=\sum_j\min\{\delta_{r,j},\hat s_{r,j}(x)\}$, and each
candidate is scored per expected teacher token,
\[
U_r(x)=\frac{\hat\rho_r(x)\,C_r(x)}{\hat c(x)}-\lambda_I\,I_r(x),
\]
where $\hat c(x)$ includes the expected cost of delivery failures,
since unverified completions are still billed, and $I_r(x)$ estimates
interference with reference capabilities from held-out reference-task
gradients as detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:method}. The capped
coverage term gives diminishing returns as directions saturate. We
greedily purchase feasible candidates with positive marginal utility,
update $\hat s$, $\hat\rho$, and the deficits with each observed
completion, and stop acquiring when the budget is exhausted or
$\max_x U_r(x)\le 0$. The budget is an upper bound, not a prescribed
expenditure.

\subsection{Teacher-scaffolded student exploration}\label{sec:explore}

Acquired demonstrations are not added to the training set. For each
query $x$ we first draw $K$ trajectories from the unguided policy
$\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x)$. If any succeeds, collection for $x$
remains unguided. If all $K$ fail and a context $h(d_x)$ has been
acquired, we draw $K_g$ additional trajectories from the guided
behavior policy $\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x,h(d_x))$. Either way,
every collected trajectory is generated by the student; the teacher
changes the exploration distribution, never the optimization target.

The rationale is a property of self-generation. Unguided samples
satisfy
$\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}
[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)]=0$, so they carry no
systematic pull toward any external distribution, and weighting them
by the verifier recovers the policy gradient of task success,
$\nabla_\theta J_x(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}
[V(x,\tau)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)]$ for
$J_x(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}[V(x,\tau)]$.
Teacher context is needed only in the cold-start regime where
$V{=}1$ samples are vanishingly rare. Because unguided attempts come
first, guidance usage adaptively tracks student competence up to
sampling noise in the $K$ rollouts, and collection shifts from
guided-heavy to unguided without an externally specified annealing
schedule.

\subsection{Learning from student-provenance trajectories}\label{sec:distill}

All collected trajectories are treated as off-policy samples from a
single recorded behavior policy $\mu_r$, which equals
$\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x)$ for unguided collection and
$\pi_{\theta_r}(\cdot\mid x,h(d_x))$ for guided collection. This
uniform treatment matters because multi-step optimization moves
$\pi_\theta$ away from the collection-time policy, so even unguided
samples become off-policy after the first update; their importance
ratio is exactly one at the first step and drifts thereafter.

\paragraph{Positive verifier objective.}
Under absolute continuity of $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ with respect
to $\mu_r(\cdot\mid x)$, importance sampling gives
$\nabla_\theta J_x(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\mu_r}
[w_\theta(\tau,x)V(x,\tau)\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)]$
with $w_\theta(\tau,x)=\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x)/\mu_r(\tau\mid x)$.
Contextual guidance does not guarantee this condition, since a
context can make some unguided-reachable trajectories unreachable, so
we read the identity as the idealized objective on overlapping
support, and we clip,
$\bar w_\theta(\tau,x)=\min\{w_\theta(\tau,x),c\}$, trading bias for
variance without restoring missing support. The trained loss is
defined explicitly with a stop-gradient on the weight, so that
automatic differentiation yields the intended estimator,
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}(\theta)
=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_r|}
\sum_{(x,\tau)\in\mathcal{B}_r}
\mathrm{sg}\bigl[\bar w_\theta(\tau,x)\,V(x,\tau)\bigr]\,
\log\pi_\theta(\tau\mid x),
\]
where $\mathcal{B}_r$ contains the collected student trajectories and
teacher-generated text receives zero weight by construction.

\paragraph{Localized negative signal.}
Failures inform learning only through outcome-contrastive pairs. For
a successful $\tau^+$ and failed $\tau^-$ sharing the environment
history up to their first action divergence
$t^\star=\min\{t:a_t^+\neq a_t^-\}$, preference supervision applies
from $t^\star$ onward. Writing $y^+$ and $y^-$ for the post-divergence
segments given the shared context $x_{<t^\star}$, the preference loss
is the reference-free odds-ratio objective \citep{hong2024orpo},
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}(\theta)
=-\log\sigma\!\Bigl(
\log\tfrac{p_\theta(y^+\mid x_{<t^\star})}{1-p_\theta(y^+\mid x_{<t^\star})}
-\log\tfrac{p_\theta(y^-\mid x_{<t^\star})}{1-p_\theta(y^-\mid x_{<t^\star})}
\Bigr),
\]
computed over admitted pairs only. A pair is admitted when the
length-normalized negative log-likelihood of $\tau^+$, evaluated in
the unguided deployment context even if $\tau^+$ was collected under
guidance, falls below an exponential-moving-average threshold, which
prevents negative pressure from dominating before the student can
represent the verified alternative
\citep{razin2025unintentional}. The full objective is
$\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{pref}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pref}}$.

\subsection{Training and deployment}\label{sec:behavior}

\paragraph{Training loop and stopping.}
Each round alternates acquisition, collection, and optimization, then
re-estimates deficits, unlock probabilities, and utilities under the
updated student. Acquisition stopping and training stopping are
distinct. Acquisition stops when no candidate retains positive
utility or the budget is exhausted, but training continues with the
already-acquired contexts and with unguided self-improvement until
the unguided convergence criterion is met. Exhausting the budget
never terminates learning; it only ends new purchases.

\paragraph{Deployment.}
After training, all parameters defining the routing score are frozen,
and $\mathcal{S}_c$ calibrates a split-conformal threshold
$\gamma_\alpha$ on the prompt-side nonconformity score $R(x)$
\citep{angelopoulos2023conformal}. Queries with
$R(x)\le\gamma_\alpha$ are served by the unguided specialist
$\pi_{\theta^\star}(\cdot\mid x)$ and the remainder are rejected or
escalated. The construction is a calibrated in-task acceptance region
under exchangeability, not an out-of-distribution detection
guarantee. Teacher context is never required at deployment.
