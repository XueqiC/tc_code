\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}\label{sec:setting}

Following the classical few-shot learning setup
\citep{vinyals2016matching, snell2017prototypical, finn2017maml,
brown2020language}, where a task is presented only through a small
support set and performance is measured on a disjoint query set, we
formally introduce the problem of budgeted few-shot agent
specialization. An agent executes a trajectory
$\tau=(a_1,o_1,\ldots,a_H,o_H)$ of actions and environment
observations, and a task verifier $V(x,\tau)\in\{0,1\}$ judges
whether $\tau$ solves $x$.

\begin{definition}[Budgeted few-shot agent specialization]
\label{def:setting}
Given a support set $\mathcal{S}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ of queries drawn
from a task distribution $\mathcal{D}$, a small student policy
$\pi_{\theta_0}$, and query access to a stronger black-box teacher
policy $\pi_\phi$ billed per emitted token under a budget $B$, we
aim to adapt the student so that the resulting agent maximizes task
performance on a disjoint query set from $\mathcal{D}$,
\[
\max_{\theta}\;
\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}}\,
\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[V(x,\tau)\bigr]
\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
\textstyle\sum_{q\in\mathcal{A}}c(q)\le B,
\]
where $\mathcal{A}$ is the set of teacher requests purchased during
training and $c(q)$ their token cost. Every teacher call is charged,
including completions that fail verification.
\end{definition}

The teacher exposes text only. For a state $s$ (the task prompt plus
the history executed so far) it returns a continuation $y^T$ drawn
from an unknown distribution, at a cost per emitted token, with no
logits, hidden states, or gradients. The student, in contrast, exposes
its full distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)$ over complete actions.
The conventional approach uses a purchased completion directly as an
imitation target, which moves the student toward the teacher as a
whole; the update cannot tell which of the existing behaviors of the
student should give way to the teacher and which should be kept. Our
method, \emph{return-targeted distillation} (RTD), keeps the teacher
text as supervision but installs it through a \emph{source-to-teacher
replacement}. At every supervised state the student first exhibits its
own behaviors by sampling, each sampled behavior receives its own
replacement coefficient, and the coefficients of a whole training
step are chosen jointly by the realized learning return of the
student update. Three coupled decisions follow, namely what to buy,
how to use it, and when to stop, and
Sections~\ref{sec:acquire}--\ref{sec:behavior} treat them in turn.
Before training we partition $\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_d\cup\mathcal{S}_c$;
only $\mathcal{S}_d$ enters acquisition and optimization, and
$\mathcal{S}_c$ is held out for certification after the student is
frozen. Within $\mathcal{S}_d$ the support tasks are split into two
folds that rotate between rounds, an \emph{inner} fold that supplies
states for supervision and a \emph{feedback} fold that supplies only
task returns and never enters a gradient.

\subsection{Task-aware teacher acquisition}\label{sec:atoms}
\label{sec:acquire}

Teacher supervision is bought as \emph{request packages}
$q=(s,L,\bar c,\kappa)$, consisting of a state, a length cap, a public
cost bound, and a category; the response of the teacher and its true
cost are revealed only after payment. In the sealed-replay protocol
used here the pool of candidate packages, their responses, and their
recorded costs are frozen in advance, the selector sees only the
public view, and a hard ledger charges the recorded cost at reveal
time, so that a purchased failure costs exactly what it cost the
teacher to produce. Purchases happen in \emph{decision windows}, with
at most $K$ packages per window under the remaining authorization
$B_t$, and the budget is a cap rather than a prescribed expenditure.

\paragraph{Value of evidence as the gain of the same teaching problem.}
Purchased evidence is consumed by one student update together with
everything already owned, so a package is valued by how much it
improves what the whole evidence set can achieve, not by an
independent insertion score. Section~\ref{sec:distill} defines, for an
evidence set $\mathcal{D}$, a local \emph{teaching problem} whose
optimal value $F^{\star}(\mathcal{D})$ is the best predicted learning
gain of one update over a common reference. The value of a candidate
purchase $Q$ is the expected improvement of that optimum,
\[
A(Q)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[F^{\star}(\mathcal{D}\cup\mathrm{Ev}(Q))
-F^{\star}(\mathcal{D})\;\big|\;\text{pre-purchase information}\bigr].
\]
The comparison holds the current student, the return gradient, the
reference point, the exposure plan, and the weights of the old
evidence fixed, and the new evidence enters only as additional
replacement directions. New evidence can therefore always be assigned
zero use, in which case the old problem and its update are recovered
exactly, and the value counts the teaching that the new package alone
makes possible, including the case in which the two sampled behaviors
of the student coincide and only the teacher can move the state. Since
the problem below is convex and its feasible set is nested, the local
optimum never decreases when evidence is added; no such guarantee is
claimed for the true task return.

\paragraph{Pre-purchase prediction and batch purchase.}
Unpurchased responses are invisible, so $A(Q)$ is predicted from
pre-purchase information only, namely the public features of the
package, freely available student behavior at its state, the request
cap, and a summary of the current teaching problem, the remaining
budget, and the evidence already owned. After a purchase the realized
directions and the realized value of the same problem are recorded as
labels for the predictor, and prediction quality is validated on
purchases grouped by parent task rather than on the candidates the
predictor itself selected. Each window solves a budgeted batch
selection under $\sum_q\hat c(q)\le B_t$ and $|\mathcal{A}_t|\le K$,
executes it through the ledger in a fixed deterministic order,
reserving at the public cap, revealing and settling at the recorded
cost, and builds the training batch only from what was actually
acquired. The control arm replaces the prediction by a random draw
under the same budget, cap, and cost model.

\subsection{Exposure units and student-provenance sources}\label{sec:explore}

Supervision is organized in \emph{exposure units}. One unit is the
teacher record of one state together with $m$ \emph{source actions}
$Y_{i1},\ldots,Y_{im}$ drawn fresh from the student snapshot $p_t$
frozen at the start of the round ($m=2$ throughout); a commit step
trains on $E$ units, at most $K$ of them from newly acquired packages
and the rest from the accumulated pool, in micro-batches of four
states. The source actions are complete behaviors of the student
itself at the supervised state, sampled at temperature one with the
true termination token or the true cap, never cached across steps,
and never filtered by format or correctness. The evidence weight $w_i$
of each unit ($\sum_i w_i=1$) is fixed before the sources are sampled
and stays frozen for the step. Before any purchase the units are
reference-pool states without teacher evidence, for which every
replacement coefficient is zero and only the distribution of the
student itself is preserved, so training does not wait for evidence.

\subsection{Learning from student-provenance trajectories}\label{sec:distill}

\paragraph{Empirical target and replacement coefficients.}
For a unit with teacher text $y_i^T$ and sources $Y_{ij}$, each
source carries a coefficient $a_{ij}\in[0,1]$, the share of that
source handed to the teacher demonstration. The empirical target of
the unit is
\[
\hat q_i=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\bigl[(1-a_{ij})\,\delta_{Y_{ij}}
+a_{ij}\,\delta_{y_i^T}\bigr],
\]
a probability distribution for every legal $a$, with the mass of
repeated behaviors merged. For $m=2$ the teacher dose
$\alpha_i=(a_{i1}+a_{i2})/2$ and the selection
$d_i=(a_{i1}-a_{i2})/2$ are read off as statistics; neither is a
separate decision. The coefficients are chosen jointly for all units
of a step by the procedure below, and they are frozen, together with
the samples and the teacher text, when the gradient is taken.

\paragraph{Estimator.}
Let $G_{ij}$ and $G_i^T$ be the gradients of the student loss on the
source $Y_{ij}$ and on the teacher text, and $\bar G_i$ the average of
the $G_{ij}$. The gradient of the frozen empirical target is
\[
g_i^{\mathrm{raw}}=\bar G_i+\frac{1}{m}\sum_j a_{ij}\,(G_i^T-G_{ij}),
\qquad
g_i^{\mathrm{CV}}=G_i^{\mathrm{soft}}+\frac{1}{m}\sum_j a_{ij}\,(G_i^T-G_{ij}),
\]
and the second form, which we use, replaces the sampled retention term
$\bar G_i$ by a soft retention gradient $G_i^{\mathrm{soft}}$ that
integrates over the next-token distribution of the snapshot along the
sampled prefixes. The two forms differ by $G_i^{\mathrm{soft}}-\bar G_i$,
a term that carries no coefficient, so their unconditional
expectations agree even though the coefficients depend on the very
samples and on the feedback (Proposition~\ref{prop:target}). When the
loss is normalized by the realized length of each sequence, the naive
prefix term is biased, and $G^{\mathrm{soft}}$ is the length-corrected
retention estimator of Appendix~\ref{app:method}, which keeps the
identity at a scale fixed before sampling. The soft term vanishes
exactly at $\pi_\theta=p_t$ under total-token normalization; under
per-sequence normalization it cannot vanish and match at once, and we
keep the matching form.

\paragraph{Teacher-conditioned directions and the joint problem.}
With the preconditioner $P$ frozen for the step, the update is affine
in the coefficients,
\[
\theta^{+}(a)=\theta-\eta P\sum_i w_iG_i^{\mathrm{soft}}+Ua,
\qquad
b_{ij}=-\frac{\eta w_i}{m}\,P\,(G_i^T-G_{ij}),
\]
where the columns of $U$ are the directions $b_{ij}$, each the
replacement of one concrete student behavior by one concrete teacher
demonstration. Identical sources give identical columns, a source that
equals the teacher gives a zero column, and a zero student gradient
with a non-zero teacher gradient still leaves a direction to learn
from; no direction is clustered, orthogonalized, or pre-filtered. A
reference coefficient vector $a^{\mathrm{ref}}$, the same for every
state and treated as an initialization constant, defines the reference
student $\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}=\theta^{+}(a^{\mathrm{ref}})$, at which
the return gradient $\hat h\approx\nabla J(\theta_{\mathrm{ref}})$ is
estimated from full task rollouts on the feedback fold with a legal
baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:method}). With $x=a-a^{\mathrm{ref}}$ the
coefficients solve
\[
\max_{-a^{\mathrm{ref}}\le x\le 1-a^{\mathrm{ref}}}\;
F(x)=\hat h^{\top}Ux-\frac{\lambda}{2}\,x^{\top}Kx
-\sum_{\ell}\varepsilon_\ell|x_\ell|,
\qquad K=U^{\top}HU,
\]
a convex problem after the absolute values are lifted to auxiliary
variables, which we solve to a checked optimality tolerance. The
linear term is the predicted return response of each replacement, the
quadratic term charges replacements that move the student in shared
directions, with the full cross terms kept, and the $\ell_1$ term is
an uncertainty regularizer built from the standard errors of $\hat h$
along each direction. The zero condition of a coefficient involves the
other coefficients through $K$, so no coefficient is frozen by its own
response alone; when feedback carries no information the same problem
returns $x=0$ and the reference teaching proceeds. The return only
sets the target. The backbone receives the single distillation update
$\theta^{+}(a^{\star})$ with $P$ frozen, no gradient passes through
the solved coefficients, and no reinforcement step is taken.

\subsection{Training and deployment}\label{sec:behavior}

\paragraph{Training loop and stopping.}
Training proceeds in budget rounds. Each round consists of twelve
commit steps with four decision windows at which packages may be
purchased, and the authorization at the end of a round is a fixed
fraction of the budget denominator. A decision window follows a fixed
order. It purchases and reveals with the acquisition predictor;
freezes the batch, the weights, and the source samples; builds the
reference student on that batch; estimates the return gradient at the
reference and solves the joint problem; and commits
$\theta^{+}(a^{\star})$ on the same batch from the original
parameters and optimizer state. Between decision windows the return
gradient is reused, the directions and the uncertainty terms are
recomputed on the new samples at every step, and the age of the
feedback is recorded. Acquisition stopping and training stopping are
distinct; a window may buy nothing and train on the accumulated pool,
and exhausting the authorization never ends learning. At the end of
each round the current student is exported and evaluated under the
official protocol of the benchmark as a development result. An
amortized controller that predicts the coefficients from
pre-solve observables is a speed variant of the same problem and is
evaluated separately.

\paragraph{Deployment.}
The trained student is deployed alone under the official decoding of
the benchmark, with the same harness, tools, and prompts as every
baseline; no teacher context or teacher call is required at test
time. The calibration split $\mathcal{S}_c$ is reserved for
certification, that is, bootstrap bounds on held-out data computed
after the student is frozen, and is never used during training.
