\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Specializing a small agent from a text-only teacher is, in current
practice, an act of imitation, and imitation answers only half of the
question. A purchased demonstration says what the teacher would do at
a state; it does not say which of the existing behaviors of the
student should give way, and maximum-likelihood training replaces all
of them. Return-targeted distillation makes the replacement explicit.
The student first exhibits its own behaviors by sampling, each
behavior receives its own share to hand to the teacher demonstration,
and the shares of a whole step are chosen by the learning return of
the student through one convex problem that charges redundant
directions and discounts uncertain ones. The estimator that installs
the chosen target keeps the expectation of the target gradient under
sample-dependent shares, and the same problem prices every purchase by
the gain it adds to what the owned evidence can teach, under a ledger
that bills failed completions. Around this mechanism we built a
complete budgeted procedure with a fixed harness, a common budget cap,
and paired controls that change only the replacement decision on
identical purchased data. Beyond the specific system, the durable
contribution is the object itself. A demonstration is not a target to
be copied but a set of concrete replacements of the behaviors of the student
itself, and which of them to make is a decision that can be
delegated to the return of the student.
