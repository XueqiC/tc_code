\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Distilling an agent for a deployment is, in current practice, an act of
faith: the task domain is implicit, the boundary is guessed from surface
text, and the student's competence is audited only by aggregate scores. We
have argued that all three problems become tractable in one space. The
gradient a trajectory induces at the student records what learning that task
would change; sparse decomposition factors this record into capability
atoms; and boundaries, capability predictions, and data selection are three
operations on the same object, with conformal calibration supplying the
statistical guarantee. Pilot evidence supports each component, including a
pre-registered proxy failure that the final design absorbs rather than
hides. Full-scale sweeps and agent-environment evaluations will determine
how far the specification-first view of distillation carries.
