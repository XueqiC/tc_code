\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Specializing a small agent from a text-only teacher is, in current
practice, an act of imitation, and imitation answers only half of the
question. A purchased demonstration says what the teacher would do at
a state; it does not say which of the student's own behaviors should
give way, and maximum-likelihood training replaces them all. Return-
targeted distillation separates the two decisions. A gate fixes, before
sampling, how much teacher mass a state receives, two fresh student
samples define the behaviors that can give way, and the student's own
learning return, measured after a virtual update on the same batch,
decides which one does, so that what the two behaviors share is left
untouched and only their difference is decided. The decision is made
jointly across the evidence of a step with an explicit charge for
redundancy, and the same problem prices every purchase by the marginal
gain it adds to the full update of the evidence already owned, under
a ledger that bills failed completions. Around this mechanism we built
a complete budgeted procedure with a fixed harness, a common budget
cap, and paired controls that change only the replacement decision on
identical purchased data. Beyond the specific system, the durable
contribution is the separation itself: how much to move and what to
move away from are different decisions, and only the second can be
delegated to the student's own return.
