\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Specializing a small agent from a strong teacher is, in current
practice, an act of imitation, and we find that imitation is the wrong
channel. Used as training targets, teacher trajectories move a small
student toward the teacher's behavior distribution faster than toward
the task, and every imitation-based baseline we evaluate ends below
the student it started from. The same demonstrations, moved from the
target into the context, unlock successful behavior the student then
produces itself, and verifier-filtered self-generation converts that
behavior into the only update whose systematic drift is the task
success gradient. Around this channel we built a complete budgeted
procedure, where a gradient-space demand representation prices each
demonstration by the coverage it is expected to unlock, guidance is
invoked only after unguided failure and disappears as queries
graduate, guided discoveries are importance-corrected toward the
deployment policy, failures teach only through outcome-localized
contrasts, and a held-out conformal boundary certifies where the
specialist may be trusted. Under matched budgets, this procedure is
the only method in our comparison that improves on its base student.
Beyond the specific system, the diagnostic view seems the durable
contribution, since measuring the task-extraneous component of any
supervision-induced update turns the choice among supervision formats
from a convention into a prediction the data can test.
