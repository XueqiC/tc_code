\section{Introduction}
%% Four-paragraph arc per PI 2026-08-12: need -> two requirements with SOTA
%% weaknesses -> our method with figure anchors -> contributions.

Enterprises are moving language-model agents out of demonstrations and
into production, where an agent typically holds one job: querying a
database, operating a fixed set of tools, automating one workflow
\citep{yao2023react, schick2023toolformer, trivedi2024appworld, yao2024taubench, zhou2024webarena, jimenez2024swebench, mialon2024gaia}. Yet serving a frontier model for a single narrow job is rarely
economical, and so practice converges on
distilling the teacher's behavior on that job into a small student
\citep{hinton2015distilling, kim2016sequence, mukherjee2023orca, fu2023specializing, gu2024minillm, agarwal2024gkd, kang2025distilling, tang2026smartad}. The job itself, however, is almost never specified formally; what the
deployer actually has is a few dozen
example queries from the intended workload. The teacher, meanwhile, is in
practice a closed commercial model reachable only through a metered API:
its weights, logits, and internals are out of reach, and every
demonstration token it emits is billed. Between those few examples, that
black-box teacher, and a deployment someone is willing to trust lies the
entire distance this paper sets out to close.

Doing so requires answering two questions: the task's boundary must be
determined from the limited examples alone, and the agent built on the
distilled student must possess capability that ends exactly at that
boundary. Neither is trivial. For the first, existing methods draw the
boundary wherever data is easiest to compare, by retrieval or
out-of-distribution detection in text-embedding space
\citep{hendrycks2017baseline, liu2020energy, geifman2017selective,
li2026scope} or by gradient influence against a single mean target
\citep{koh2017understanding, pruthi2020estimating, park2023trak,
engstrom2024dsdm, xia2024less}, and so can say what resembles or helps the
examples but never where the task ends; identical query texts solved in
two styles already defeat every text-side boundary
(Figure~\ref{fig:one}a). For the second, state-of-the-art agent
distillation \citep{tang2026smartad, liu2025structured, kang2025distilling}
refines how faithfully each trace is imitated, yet even given a perfect
boundary it neither controls nor measures where the token budget goes:
equally accurate students spend a large share of it acquiring capability
the job never asked for, and that diverted spending, not the extra
capability, is what the deployer pays for (Figure~\ref{fig:one}c).

Our starting point is a change of representation with a satisfying
consequence: both questions turn out to share one answer. The gradient a
teacher demonstration induces at the student's initialization records what
this student would have to learn from it, and across a task these learning
demands are not arbitrary; they are combinations of a small set of
recurring directions, the way recipes are combinations of a small set of
basic techniques. A sparse dictionary \citep{olshausen1996emergence} recovers them as \emph{capability atoms}, the learning-demand analogue of the sparse features that dictionary methods extract from network activations \citep{huben2024sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, templeton2024scaling};
sparsity is what makes the recovery meaningful, because a principal
subspace is invariant to rotations of its basis and thus cannot attach
identity to any direction, whereas rotation destroys code sparsity, so
atoms lock onto self-contained recurring skills. Atoms resolve skills
within a single domain at AUROC $0.906$ where dense subspaces reach
$0.615$ (Figure~\ref{fig:one}b). In this vocabulary distillation stops being a ranking problem and
becomes bookkeeping: the example queries define a demand ledger with a
conformal admission test calibrated on the queries themselves, every
candidate trace defines a per-token supply schedule, and selection
repeatedly takes the admitted trace satisfying the largest remaining
demand per token, charging the ledger as it goes. Budget therefore flows
to skills with unsatisfied demand and never to out-of-scope ones: the student reaches
target in-task performance with less budget, and off-task capability stays
at zero at every budget while the strongest baseline converts every
surplus token into out-of-scope capability (Figure~\ref{fig:one}c,d).
Finally, because gradient geometry proves reliable for selecting data but
unreliable for judging competence, training keeps the checkpoint that
behaves best on held-out specification queries, and the deployed agent
ships with finite-sample Clopper--Pearson certificates on both sides of
the boundary, behind a conformal gate that routes live requests.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}[left=0pt,itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Problem formulation.} We formalize
\emph{distill-to-specification}: from a teacher agent and $k$ example
queries, produce a boundary predictor with finite-sample coverage together
with a student agent whose measured capability matches the specified
domain on both sides, evaluated by budget-to-target curves and certified
leakage under matched token budgets.
\item \textbf{Key insight.} Task identity and task boundary live in the
space of learning demands: gradients separate what text cannot, and their
recurring structure is identifiable as capability atoms precisely because
of sparsity, not subspace geometry. A separation principle with
pre-registered evidence, geometry selects while behavior judges, dictates
where each signal may be used.
\item \textbf{Method.} Market-clearing distillation over capability atoms:
demand ledgers, per-token supply schedules, a calibrated admission test,
and endogenous diminishing returns, with behaviorally timed training and
certified two-sided deployment; every threshold is a statistical
convention or learned from the task's own calibration data.
\item \textbf{Results.} On a controlled five-domain suite the method
matches or beats the strongest influence-based selection in-task at every
size and budget (all wins at the canonical budget across four student
sizes), with 3 to 10 times lower variance, zero off-task residue where
baselines leak $0.40$ to $0.77$, and target performance at a fraction of
the budget on scoped tasks; the pipeline transfers unchanged to agent
benchmarks.
\end{itemize}
